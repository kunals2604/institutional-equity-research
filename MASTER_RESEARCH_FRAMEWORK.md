# 0. CORE OBJECTIVE

Conduct an **extremely deep, comprehensive, evidence-based, institutional-quality investment analysis of [COMPANY NAME]** because I am evaluating whether it is worth investing in **at the current market price and under the current business conditions**.

This is NOT a generic stock-analysis report.

The objective is to determine:

> **What exactly is this business, how economically attractive is it, how strong is its financial position, how capable and credible is management, what competitive advantages exist, what future value-creation paths are possible, what could permanently impair the business, what the market already expects, and whether the current price provides an attractive risk-adjusted opportunity.**

The analysis must be capable of identifying four fundamentally different types of investment opportunities:

1. **Layer 1 — Bedrock Compounder**
2. **Layer 2 — High-Growth Business**
3. **Layer 3 — Cyclical / Event-Driven Opportunity**
4. **Layer 4 — Venture-Style / Optionality Bet**

## CRITICAL ARCHITECTURAL RULE

**Do NOT classify the company into one layer before analyzing it.**

Instead:

> **Analyze the company independently through ALL FOUR lenses first.**

Only after completing all four analyses should you determine:

- Which layer best explains the company
- Whether a secondary layer also applies
- Whether the company is a hybrid
- Whether none of the four frameworks fits particularly well

This is essential to avoid anchoring bias.

A company that initially appears to be a compounder may actually be a cyclical opportunity. A company that appears mediocre on mature-business metrics may be an exceptional high-growth opportunity. A company with weak current economics may contain valuable venture-style optionality.

**Do not pre-classify the company. Investigate first, classify second.**
# CONTINUOUS EXECUTION RULE — DO NOT STOP BETWEEN PHASES

The phases in this framework are internal research milestones, NOT stopping points.

Do NOT finish a phase, report that phase, and wait for another instruction before continuing.
Do NOT stop after source extraction.
Do NOT stop after data validation.
Do NOT stop after financial analysis.
Do NOT stop after qualitative analysis.
Do NOT ask for permission to proceed to the next phase.

Execute the entire workflow continuously:

> SOURCE DISCOVERY → EXTRACTION → VALIDATION → HISTORICAL ANALYSIS → BUSINESS ANALYSIS → MANAGEMENT / GOVERNANCE ANALYSIS → RISK / RED-FLAG ANALYSIS → VALUATION → SCENARIOS → SCORING → FINAL VERDICT

The phase structure exists only to keep the work organized and auditable.

If a source is unavailable, incomplete, blocked, or technically difficult to retrieve:
1. Continue with all other available research.
2. Search for the same information through other credible sources where possible.
3. Record the limitation.
4. Do NOT stop the overall workflow.
5. Complete the final report using the strongest evidence actually available.

Never return a response such as “Phase 1 complete; tell me when to continue.”
Only stop if a fundamental technical limitation makes meaningful analysis impossible.

# 8. CRITICAL INTERPRETATION OF THE TWO SCORES

Examples:

### 95 Business Quality + 48 Investment Attractiveness

> **Exceptional business, but currently too expensive → WATCHLIST / WAIT.**

### 75 Business Quality + 89 Investment Attractiveness

> **Not an elite compounder, but an unusually attractive cyclical/event opportunity → STRONG BUY (or BUY if IA confidence is Low).**

### 68 Business Quality + 82 Investment Attractiveness

> **Ordinary-looking business with unusually attractive valuation/catalysts → a buy-rated opportunity, typically Layer 3 primary. The verdict follows the mapping; the special-situation character is described by the layer, not by a separate verdict label.**

### 55 Business Quality + 42 Investment Attractiveness

> **Weak business and unattractive price → AVOID.**

This distinction is mandatory.
