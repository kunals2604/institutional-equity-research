# Sector Playbooks

Expands `MASTER_RESEARCH_FRAMEWORK.md` §§40–45. Every specialist reads the playbook(s) for the company's sector(s). Conglomerates use one per material segment. The **"Layer questions to test"** are prompts for the four-layer analysis, **not default classifications**.

Structure of each playbook: economics, KPIs, accounting and forensic traps, cycle drivers, valuation (use / avoid), layer questions to test, sources.

---

## 1. Banks

- **Economics:** spread business funded by deposits. Returns = NIM − cost-to-income drag − credit cost, levered by capital. The liability franchise (CASA, granular retail deposits) is usually the moat; underwriting discipline protects it.
- **KPIs:** advances and deposit growth; CASA ratio; cost of funds; yield on advances; NIM; fee income share; cost-to-income; credit cost; slippage ratio; GNPA / NNPA / PCR; restructured and SMA book; write-offs and recoveries; CRAR / CET1 / Tier-1; LCR; ROA / ROE; segment mix (retail, SME, corporate, unsecured); top-20 exposure concentration; RWA density.
- **Accounting and forensic traps:** evergreening (rising restructured or SMA-2 while GNPA looks stable); technical write-offs that flatter GNPA (check PCR including write-offs); treasury gains propping up profits; divergence reports where disclosed; ever-greened large corporate accounts; aggressive recognition of interest on stressed loans; low provisioning on unsecured growth.
- **Cycle drivers:** credit cycle (2–4 years after rapid unsecured growth), rate cycle (NIM), deposit competition, regulatory risk-weight changes.
- **Valuation:** P/B vs. sustainable ROE (justified P/B = (ROE − g)/(CoE − g)); P/E on normalized credit cost; residual income. **Avoid** EV/EBITDA and FCF.
- **Layer questions to test:** L1: can it compound book value at an ROE well above its cost of equity through a credit cycle? L2: share gains in under-penetrated segments with stable asset quality? L3: a recovering credit cycle or recapitalization with mispriced normalized ROE? L4: rarely relevant (digital-bank optionality).
- **Sources:** annual-report Basel III Pillar 3 disclosures, RBI statistics, rating rationales, quarterly asset-quality disclosures.

## 2. NBFCs and housing finance companies

- **Economics:** like banks, but wholesale-funded (bonds, bank loans, CP), so funding access and ALM are existential. A large AUM share via co-lending or securitization changes risk retention.
- **KPIs:** AUM and disbursement growth by product; on-book vs. off-book; yield, cost of funds, spread, NIM; opex / AUM; credit cost; Stage-2 and Stage-3 (ECL) ratios and PCR; write-offs; collection efficiency; ALM bucket gaps (≤1 year); borrowing mix (bank / NCD / CP / ECB); CRAR and Tier-1; leverage (assets/equity); customer franchise (customers, cross-sell); branch productivity; for housing finance, LTV and ticket size.
- **Accounting and forensic traps:** Ind AS 109 ECL model assumptions (management overlays released to boost profit); upfront gains on securitization or assignment; restructured book reclassification; delinquency masked by fresh disbursements (track lagged Stage-3 = current Stage-3 / AUM 12 months ago); related-party lending within groups; CP dependence; evergreening through group entities.
- **Cycle drivers:** liquidity cycles (funding freezes), the unsecured-credit cycle, regulatory tightening (risk weights, scale-based regulation tiers), rate cycle.
- **Valuation:** P/B vs. ROE (RoA × leverage) with sustainable credit cost; P/E on normalized earnings; a growth-adjusted residual-income model. **Stress-test ROE at peak-cycle credit cost.**
- **Layer questions to test:** L1: durable underwriting with ROA above the cost of capital across cycles and a low-cost funding advantage? L2: AUM growth runway in under-penetrated credit with stable credit cost? L3: a post-crisis normalization of credit cost or funding spreads? L4: digital lending or new-product optionality with unproven loss rates.
- **Sources:** annual-report ECL and ALM notes, rating rationales (funding, ALM), RBI regulations, monthly or quarterly business updates.

## 3. Insurance (life and general)

- **Economics:** life insurers earn value of new business (VNB) and investment spread, with long-duration liabilities; general insurers earn underwriting result + float income.
- **KPIs (life):** APE growth; VNB and VNB margin; product mix (par / non-par / ULIP / protection); persistency (13th / 61st month); embedded value (EV) and operating RoEV; solvency ratio; distribution mix (bancassurance concentration); cost ratios.
- **KPIs (general):** GWP growth by line; combined ratio (loss + expense); reserve development; solvency ratio; investment yield; motor/health mix; claims settlement.
- **Traps:** EV assumption changes (discount rates, persistency, mortality) driving "growth"; under-reserving (general); bancassurance partner concentration; one-off regulatory changes to commission and expense rules.
- **Valuation:** P/EV and appraisal value (EV + multiple × VNB) for life; P/B vs. ROE and combined-ratio normalization for general. **Avoid** P/E for life insurers.
- **Layer questions to test:** L1: durable VNB growth at stable margins with a protection-heavy mix? L2: penetration-driven growth? L3: regulatory-shock mispricing?
- **Sources:** public disclosures (insurer website forms), IRDAI data, EV reports.

## 4. Asset managers, brokers, exchanges and capital-market intermediaries

- **Economics:** fee-based, with operating leverage on market levels (AMCs) or activity (brokers, exchanges). Highly correlated with market cycles.
- **KPIs:** AUM (equity share), market share, yield (bps), SIP flows, cost/AUM (AMC); active clients, market share in cash / F&O, ARPU, MTF book (brokers); ADTV, market share, clearing income, regulatory fees (exchanges).
- **Traps:** treating peak-volume earnings as normal; regulatory changes to F&O, TER (expense ratio) and lot sizes; float income dependence.
- **Valuation:** P/E on **normalized** activity, EV/EBITDA, market-share-adjusted DCF; scenario tests for regulatory caps.
- **Layer questions to test:** L1: network effects and liquidity moats (exchanges)? L2: financialization runway? L3: market-cycle trough opportunity?
- **Sources:** AMFI, exchange business-growth statistics, regulator circulars.

## 5. IT services

- **Economics:** people-based delivery; margins depend on utilization, pricing, the pyramid, offshore mix and currency. Large deals give visibility. AI and automation are a structural deflation risk to effort-based billing.
- **KPIs:** constant-currency revenue growth; deal TCV and book-to-bill; client buckets ($1m+, $50m+, $100m+); top-client concentration; vertical and geographic mix; utilization; attrition; headcount and revenue per employee; offshore share; EBIT margin and drivers (wage hikes, currency, pass-throughs); DSO including unbilled; FCF conversion; capital return policy.
- **Traps:** unbilled revenue growth; pass-through revenue inflating growth; acquisitions masking organic growth; FX hedge gains in other income; capitalized contract costs; one-off deal-renewal discounts.
- **Valuation:** P/E and FCF yield vs. growth durability; DCF with a terminal competitive fade; total shareholder yield (dividends + buybacks).
- **Layer questions to test:** L1: can it sustain high ROIC with modest growth and high payout? L2: a mid-tier share-gain story? L3: a demand-cycle trough (discretionary spending)? L4: AI-led new service lines?
- **Sources:** quarterly fact sheets and investor metrics files, annual reports, peer disclosures.

## 6. Consumer internet and software platforms

- **Economics:** network effects or scale in customer acquisition; high gross margins at maturity; early losses funded by equity.
- **KPIs:** GMV / GOV, take rate, revenue, contribution margin per order or user, CAC and payback, cohort retention, MAU/DAU, frequency, AOV; adjusted EBITDA vs. reported (ESOP cost); cash burn and runway; for SaaS, ARR, NRR / GRR, gross margin, magic number, rule of 40.
- **Traps:** adjusted metrics excluding ESOP costs (SBC is a real expense); GMV definitions; one-offs in contribution margin; capitalized software development; gross vs. net revenue recognition shifts.
- **Valuation:** scenario DCF to steady-state margins; EV/gross profit; cohort-based LTV; **probability-weighted outcomes with dilution** for Layer-4-like profiles. **Avoid** trailing P/E.
- **Layer questions to test:** L2: unit economics improving with scale? L4: which milestones prove the option? L1: do network effects create durable excess returns once mature?
- **Sources:** shareholder letters, annual reports, offer documents (cohort data), app-store or industry data (tier 5–6, with care).

## 7. Consumer staples (FMCG)

- **Economics:** brand plus distribution; low capital intensity; high ROCE; pricing power tested in input-cost inflation.
- **KPIs:** underlying volume growth vs. price/mix; gross margin vs. input-cost index (palm oil, crude derivatives, milk, wheat); A&P spend; direct distribution reach (outlets); rural vs. urban; category market shares; working capital (often negative); royalty to parent (MNCs); new-product contribution.
- **Traps:** channel inventory loading at quarter ends; trade-scheme accounting; royalty increases to foreign parents (related party); "premiumization" hiding volume decline.
- **Valuation:** P/E and DCF with long duration; a reverse DCF is essential, because these often price in decades of growth; FCF yield.
- **Layer questions to test:** L1: classic compounder test with emphasis on price paid; L3: input-cost-cycle margin normalization.
- **Sources:** annual reports, industry panel data where public (often paywalled), peer filings.

## 8. Consumer discretionary, retail, QSR and durables

- **KPIs:** same-store sales growth (SSSG); store additions and sq ft; revenue/sq ft; store-level EBITDA and payback; inventory turns; private-label share; online share; (QSR) ADS, delivery share; (durables) channel inventory, market share by category.
- **Traps:** Ind AS 116 lease effects (compare pre-Ind AS EBITDA); new-store maturation curves; franchise fees vs. company-owned; capitalized pre-operative costs.
- **Valuation:** EV/EBITDA (pre-Ind AS 116 consistent), DCF with store roll-out economics, P/E.
- **Layer questions to test:** L2: store roll-out runway with proven unit economics? L1: brand-led compounding? L3: discretionary demand cycle?
- **Sources:** company disclosures, mall and retail industry reports (tier 5).

## 9. Automobiles and auto components

- **Economics:** cyclical volume business with operating leverage; moats come from distribution, brand, product cycles and cost; the electric-vehicle transition reshapes cost structures and competition.
- **KPIs:** domestic and export volumes by segment; market share (SIAM wholesale vs. VAHAN retail); ASP and mix; EBITDA per vehicle; raw-material cost per vehicle (steel, aluminium, precious metals, cells); capacity utilization; dealer count and dealer inventory days; EV share, EV unit economics and incentive dependence; captive-finance penetration; components: content per vehicle, customer concentration, OEM share of wallet, export mix, PLI benefits.
- **Traps:** captive-finance consolidation distorting debt and ROCE (analyze ex-financing); channel stuffing (wholesale ≫ retail, rising dealer inventory); incentive and subsidy income timing (PLI, EV schemes); overseas subsidiary losses hidden in consolidation; warranty provisions.
- **Cycle drivers:** rural income and monsoon, financing availability and rates, regulation-driven price increases (emission and safety norms), commodities, export currencies.
- **Valuation:** P/E and EV/EBITDA on **mid-cycle** margins; SOTP for financing and other subsidiaries; DCF with explicit EV-transition scenarios.
- **Layer questions to test:** L1: durable share and distribution moat with through-cycle ROCE? L2: premiumization or exports? L3: volume cycle position? L4: EV or new-mobility subsidiaries.
- **Sources:** SIAM, FADA, VAHAN, company monthly volume releases, peer filings.

## 10. Pharmaceuticals and healthcare

- **Economics:** generics (price erosion, regulatory compliance), branded domestic formulations (field force, brands), specialty/complex products (pipeline), CDMO/CRAMS (client relationships, capacity, compliance), hospitals (beds, occupancy, ARPOB, payor mix), diagnostics (network, volume, price competition).
- **KPIs:** US generics price erosion and new launches; ANDA pipeline; FDA inspection status by plant (OAI, warning letters, import alerts); domestic formulation growth vs. market; field-force productivity; R&D % of sales; CDMO order book and client concentration; hospitals: ARPOB, occupancy, beds (operational vs. planned), payor mix, EBITDA per bed, ramp-up of new units.
- **Traps:** capitalized R&D; one-off exclusivity gains ("para IV" or limited-competition launches) treated as recurring; regulatory remediation costs; price-control (NLEM/DPCO) exposure; related-party trusts (hospitals).
- **Valuation:** P/E and EV/EBITDA ex-one-offs; product-level DCF for specialty pipelines (risk-adjusted NPV); EV/bed or EV/EBITDA with maturity adjustments for hospitals.
- **Layer questions to test:** L1: durable domestic branded franchise? L2: hospital expansion runway? L3: regulatory-remediation recovery (plant clearance)? L4: specialty or biosimilar pipeline.
- **Sources:** FDA databases, CDSCO and price-control notifications, company filings.

## 11. Capital goods, engineering, defence and infrastructure EPC

- **Economics:** order-book businesses with working-capital intensity; margins depend on execution, commodity pass-through clauses and bid discipline.
- **KPIs:** order inflow and book-to-bill; order book / revenue (years of visibility); order mix (government / private / export); execution rate; EBITDA margin by segment; net working capital days (including retentions and unbilled); advances from customers; bank guarantees; arbitration claims; capex cycle indicators.
- **Traps:** percentage-of-completion revenue recognition (contract assets / unbilled growth); arbitration awards booked as income; receivables from government counterparties; aggressive cost-to-complete estimates; related-party subcontracting.
- **Valuation:** P/E and EV/EBITDA vs. order-book growth; DCF with working-capital realism; SOTP for listed subsidiaries.
- **Layer questions to test:** L2: multi-year order-book growth with improving margins? L3: capex cycle upswing? L1: rarely, but possible for niche high-ROCE product companies.
- **Sources:** order announcements (exchange filings), budget documents, ministry procurement data.

## 12. Metals, mining and commodities

- **Economics:** price takers; cost-curve position decides survival; extreme cyclicality; capital intensity.
- **KPIs:** production and sales volumes; realization per tonne; EBITDA per tonne; cost per tonne by component (ore/coal, power); integration (captive mines and power); cost-curve quartile; capacity additions (company and global); net debt / EBITDA at mid-cycle; capex plans; spot vs. contract pricing.
- **Traps:** peak-cycle earnings capitalized; inventory valuation gains; hedging gains; capitalized interest on projects; related-party raw-material purchases.
- **Valuation:** EV/EBITDA on **mid-cycle** EBITDA/tonne; replacement cost per tonne; NAV for mining. **Never** peak P/E.
- **Layer questions to test:** L3: almost always the primary test (cycle position, balance-sheet survival, normalized earnings); L1 only for structural cost leaders through cycles.
- **Sources:** ministry and association data, exchange price data, peer filings.

## 13. Cement and building materials

- **Economics:** regional oligopolies; freight-cost-limited markets; capacity cycles; power and fuel costs.
- **KPIs:** capacity and utilization by region; volume growth; realization per tonne; EBITDA per tonne; cost per tonne (power and fuel, freight, raw materials); green power share; capex per tonne of new capacity; net debt; market share by region; trade vs. non-trade mix.
- **Traps:** incentive accounting (state subsidies); capitalized costs of new plants; acquisition EV/tonne overpayment.
- **Valuation:** EV/EBITDA on normalized EBITDA per tonne; EV per tonne vs. replacement cost.
- **Layer questions to test:** L3: utilization and pricing cycle; L1: cost leadership and regional dominance.
- **Sources:** company filings, industry association data.

## 14. Chemicals (specialty and commodity)

- **KPIs:** volume vs. price split; product and customer concentration; gross margin vs. raw-material spreads; capacity and utilization; capex pipeline and asset turns; R&D and new-molecule pipeline; export share and China competition; contract manufacturing share; environmental compliance.
- **Traps:** "specialty" labels on commodity products; inventory gains in raw-material price spikes; capitalized trial-run costs; capex asset turns below promises.
- **Valuation:** EV/EBITDA and P/E vs. growth and ROCE; mid-cycle spreads for commodities; DCF for contract manufacturers with customer concentration risk.
- **Layer questions to test:** L2: import substitution and China+1 runway? L3: destocking-cycle trough? L1: niche chemistry moats?
- **Sources:** customs/export data (tier 2/5), company filings, peer filings.

## 15. Oil and gas, energy and utilities (including power)

- **Economics:** regulated returns (transmission, distribution, regulated generation), commodity spreads (refining, marketing), resource economics (upstream), merchant power cycles.
- **KPIs:** regulated equity and allowed ROE; PLF and availability; PPA tenure and counterparty quality (state discoms); receivable days from discoms; refining margins (GRM) and marketing margins; upstream production and realization; capex pipeline; regulated asset base growth; renewable capacity pipeline and tariffs.
- **Traps:** regulatory-asset recognition; late-payment surcharge income; government pricing interventions; capitalized interest; related-party fuel supply.
- **Valuation:** P/B vs. regulated ROE; DCF of regulated cash flows; EV/EBITDA on normalized spreads; SOTP.
- **Layer questions to test:** L1: regulated compounding with reinvestment runway? L3: commodity-spread normalization, or regulatory change?
- **Sources:** CEA, PPAC, regulator tariff orders, company filings.

## 16. Telecom and media

- **Economics:** spectrum + network scale; high fixed costs; industry structure (number of players) decides pricing power; capex cycles with each technology generation.
- **KPIs:** subscribers (total, data, 4G/5G); ARPU; churn; data usage per user; minutes; EBITDA margin (pre/post Ind AS 116); capex / revenue; spectrum holdings and remaining payment obligations; deferred spectrum and AGR liabilities; net debt / EBITDA including leases; tower tenancy (tower companies); enterprise and home-broadband growth; Africa/other geography FX exposure. **Media:** ad revenue cycle, subscription growth, content costs and amortization.
- **Traps:** lease accounting distorting EBITDA; capitalized spectrum and its amortization; government dues (deferred payments) as quasi-debt; FX translation losses in overseas units; content cost capitalization (media).
- **Valuation:** EV/EBITDA (lease-consistent) and EV/EBITDA-minus-capex; DCF with explicit capex cycles; SOTP for tower and other listed holdings.
- **Layer questions to test:** L1: in a rational oligopoly, durable ARPU growth with falling capital intensity? L3: tariff-repair inflection or deleveraging? L4: adjacencies (data centers, payments).
- **Sources:** TRAI reports, DoT notices, company quarterly reports.

## 17. Real estate

- **Economics:** project-based; cash flows driven by pre-sales and collections; land bank and approvals are the critical inputs; leverage risk.
- **KPIs:** pre-sales (bookings) value and volume; collections; launches and pipeline; realization per sq ft; inventory months; land bank (owned vs. joint development); net debt and cash flow from operations (collections − construction − land); RERA project status; rental-portfolio occupancy and cap rates (commercial).
- **Traps:** revenue recognition on completion making P&L lag cash; JDA and land-cost accounting; capitalized interest; related-party land deals.
- **Valuation:** NAV (project DCF + land valuation − net debt), P/B (with caution); cap-rate valuation for rental assets.
- **Layer questions to test:** L3: the housing cycle and deleveraging are often primary; L2: platform developers with replicable execution and capital-light JDAs.
- **Sources:** RERA portals, company presentations, filings.

## 18. Holding companies and conglomerates

- **Economics:** value lies in the underlying operating companies; a holding discount reflects leakage, capital-allocation risk and illiquidity.
- **KPIs:** look-through earnings; SOTP of listed and unlisted holdings; holdco net debt and cash; dividend income vs. holdco costs; history of capital calls into weak subsidiaries; cross-holdings; promoter control structure.
- **Traps:** double counting through cross-holdings; unlisted stakes at stale values; intra-group loans and guarantees; dividend upstreaming that strains subsidiaries.
- **Valuation:** SOTP with a **justified** holding discount (not a default 20–40 %), plus a mechanism for the discount to close (special-situations).
- **Layer questions to test:** L3: discount-closure catalysts (simplification, buybacks, listings); L1: is the capital allocator genuinely compounding look-through value?
- **Sources:** each subsidiary's filings, AOC-1, scheme documents.

---

## Cross-sector reminders

- Use the **economically appropriate basis**: industrial ex-financing, lender metrics for lenders, pre-lease EBITDA for lease-heavy models.
- **Mid-cycle normalization** applies to every cyclical sector above.
- **Regulatory dependence** (price controls, tariffs, licences, incentive schemes) is a structural-risk factor (BQ8) in every sector where it exists.
