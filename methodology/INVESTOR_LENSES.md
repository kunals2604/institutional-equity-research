# Analytical Lenses — Respected Investors' Methods as Explicit Tests

These lenses come from publicly documented principles. Each is written as a **test**: a question, the evidence needed, and what failure looks like. Reports name the *test*, not the investor. An investor's reputation is never evidence that a conclusion is right. Where lenses disagree for a company, the report records the disagreement and says which test matters most for that company's layer.

| # | Test | Origin (public principle) | Question | Evidence required | Failure signal | Most relevant layers | Owner(s) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| T1 | **Business-research (scuttlebutt) test** | Fisher: research products, management depth, sales organization, R&D and long-run growth | Do customers, competitors, suppliers and the company's own operating data corroborate the growth and quality story? | Operating KPIs, customer and channel evidence, competitor filings, product reviews, regulatory data. **Never claim interviews that did not happen.** | Story supported only by management | L1, L2 | **scuttlebutt** (primary); industry, moat, management |
| T2 | **Owner-earnings test** | Buffett: owner earnings = earnings + D&A − maintenance capex ± working-capital needs | What cash could an owner take out while preserving competitive position? | CFO, capex split, working capital, maintenance-capex estimate with basis | Reported profit persistently above owner earnings | L1, L3 | financials, valuation |
| T3 | **Incremental-return test** | Buffett / Mukherjea: returns on *incremental* capital matter more than the average | What did the last 5–10 years of retained capital earn? | ΔNOPAT ÷ Δinvested capital over rolling windows; per-share value growth vs. retained earnings | Retained earnings growing faster than per-share value | L1, L2 | financials |
| T4 | **Inversion test** | Munger: "invert, always invert" | What would make this a bad investment even if the next few quarters are good? Which single assumption, if wrong, does the most damage? | A list of thesis-critical assumptions and their evidence | No credible answer, or an answer that is ignored | All | bear-case, CIO |
| T5 | **Incentive test** | Munger: incentives drive behavior | Do management's pay, ownership and related-party arrangements reward per-share value creation for minority holders? | Remuneration structure, pledges, related-party flows, ESOP terms | Pay tied to size or standalone profit while capital leaks elsewhere | All | management, filings |
| T6 | **Opportunity-cost test** | Buffett / Munger | Is this better than the best alternative use of capital at a similar risk level? | Hurdle rate, E[IRR], comparison with simple alternatives | E[IRR] below hurdle presented as acceptable | All | valuation, CIO |
| T7 | **Clean-accounts and capital-efficiency test** | Mukherjea: consistency, clean accounting, capital efficiency (sustained high ROCE, revenue growth) | Are the accounts clean, and have returns and growth been *consistently* high through difficult years? | Year-by-year ROCE and revenue growth across ≥10 years where available; CFO/PAT; auditor and related-party record | Consistency claimed from averages that hide bad years; quality inferred from brand or promoter reputation | L1 | financials, filings |
| T8 | **Cycle-position test** | Marks: know where you are in the cycle; risk means permanent loss, not volatility | Where are earnings, margins, capacity and sentiment relative to the cycle? What is priced in? | Peak, trough and mid-cycle margins and returns; capacity additions; credit conditions; valuation vs. history | Peak earnings capitalized at peak multiples | L3 (all) | industry, special-situations, valuation |
| T9 | **Second-level-thinking test** | Marks | What does the consensus already believe, and why might it be wrong? | Reverse valuation, consensus estimates (tagged ESTIMATE), sentiment indicators | "Great company" offered as the reason to buy | All | valuation, CIO |
| T10 | **Value-realization-mechanism test** | Greenblatt: special situations need a mechanism | By what specific mechanism, by when, and with what probability does value reach the share price? | Board approvals, scheme documents, timelines, capital-return policies | A catalyst with no realization mechanism; an unannounced event treated as probable | L3 | special-situations |
| T11 | **Know-what-you-own / category test** | Lynch: classify (slow, stalwart, fast grower, cyclical, turnaround, asset play) and explain the story in two minutes | Can the thesis be stated plainly, and does the evidence fit the category's typical behavior? | A plain-language thesis; category base behaviors | Thesis needs jargon or many conditions to hold | All | CIO |
| T12 | **Expectations-investing test** | Mauboussin: reverse-engineer the price | What revenue growth, margin, reinvestment and competitive-advantage period does the price imply? | Reverse DCF with stated WACC and terminal assumptions | Implied expectations never compared with base rates | L1, L2 | valuation |
| T13 | **Base-rate test** | Mauboussin / Kahneman: outside view | How often have companies in this reference class achieved the growth, margins or turnaround assumed? | A cited reference class where available; otherwise an explicit qualitative band and reasoning | Inside-view projection with no reference class; percentages with no basis | L2, L3, L4 | valuation, bear-case |
| T14 | **Narrative-and-numbers test** | Damodaran: every number should map to a story, and every story to a number | Does each valuation input follow from the business narrative, and is the narrative possible, plausible and probable? | Input-by-input linkage from story to model | Inputs chosen to hit a target value; TAM accepted without a reachable-market argument | L2, L4 | valuation |
| T15 | **Lifecycle-fit test** | Damodaran: corporate lifecycle | Is the valuation method suited to the company's lifecycle stage? | Stage assessment; method selection rationale | Mature DCF applied to an early-stage business, or growth multiples to a declining one | All | valuation |
| T16 | **Power-law / dilution test** | Venture capital practice | What is the distribution of outcomes, how much capital is needed, and what dilution precedes success? What is per-share value after financing? | Runway, burn, financing history, milestone map, probability-weighted outcomes | Success case valued without the dilution needed to reach it; failure probability omitted | L4 | valuation, financials, special-situations |
| T17 | **Signal-vs-noise test** | Empirical / quantitative discipline (publicly described principles of systematic investors such as Simons; no proprietary methods are claimed) | Is an apparent pattern (a margin trend, quarter, or correlation) large relative to its normal variation and supported by enough observations? | Multi-year series, variance, number of observations; YoY over QoQ | Conclusions drawn from one or two quarters or from cherry-picked periods | All | financials, concalls, CIO |

## How layers change the questions

The four layers demand different decisions, not the same analysis under four labels:

| Layer | Primary tests | Decisive evidence | Typical error |
|---|---|---|---|
| L1 Bedrock Compounder | T2, T3, T5, T7, T12 | Through-cycle incremental returns, reinvestment runway, moat mechanism, governance | Paying any price for quality; using peak returns |
| L2 High-Growth Business | T1, T12, T13, T14, T15 | Unit economics, retention, reachable market, path to FCF, dilution | Treating TAM as value; rejecting on trailing multiples |
| L3 Cyclical / Event-Driven Opportunity | T8, T9, T10, T13 | Normalized earnings, trough survival, catalyst mechanism and probability, downside floor | Peak earnings at peak multiples; catalysts without mechanisms; value traps |
| L4 Venture-Style / Optionality Bet | T13, T14, T16 | Milestones, runway, dilution-adjusted outcome tree, probability of near-total loss | Ignoring failure and dilution; confusing an imaginable TAM with realizable value |

When the lenses point in different directions, the CIO records the disagreement in the final report under the relevant layer. For example, T7 may favor a company while T8 says its returns are at a cyclical peak. The CIO then states which test governs for the chosen primary layer.
