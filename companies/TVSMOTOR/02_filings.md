## 1. Executive Findings

1. **FACT** — Both TVS Motor's standalone and consolidated FY26 financial statements carry **unmodified (clean) audit opinions** from the statutory auditors, and no auditor qualifications, adverse opinions, disclaimers, or emphasis-of-matter paragraphs were identified in any year reviewed (FY23–FY26). This corroborates the Financials report's finding.
2. **FACT** — TVS Motor's statutory auditor **changed during FY2023-24**: V. Sankar Aiyar & Co. (Mumbai) audited the standalone accounts from at least FY2008 through FY2022-23; Sundaram & Srinivasan (Chennai) has audited both standalone and consolidated accounts from FY2023-24 onward. **CALCULATION/INFERENCE** — cross-referencing AGM notices, V. Sankar Aiyar & Co.'s "first term of five years" began around 2013 (per the 2015 AGM notice describing that year as "the third year in the first term of five years"); a second five-year term would run to roughly 2023. This is **consistent with, not a violation of, the Companies Act 2013 Section 139(2) mandatory audit-firm rotation cap of two consecutive five-year terms (10 years)** for listed companies — i.e., the change appears to be routine, compliant rotation rather than a forced resignation or a response to an audit dispute. No public disclosure of a qualified resignation, disagreement, or SEBI/MCA action tied to this rotation was found.
3. **FACT** — The statutory auditors' Key Audit Matter has been **"Government Grants" (PLI-scheme recognition) in at least three consecutive years (FY24, FY25, FY26)**, in near-identical wording each year, for both standalone and consolidated statements. **INFERENCE** — a Key Audit Matter that recurs unchanged for three straight years indicates the auditors continue to regard PLI-linked revenue/grant-income recognition as a persistently significant judgment area — this corroborates, from an independent primary source, the Financials report's own flag (Section 10) that Q4 FY25's 14.0% EBITDA margin spike was PLI-driven and required "normalisation."
4. **RED FLAG (independently sourced, extends the Financials report)** — TVS Motor's own FY26 Annual Report states cumulative investment in Norton Motorcycles of **"over £250 million to date"** (as of the FY26 AR, published ~June–August 2026). Independent UK Companies House filings, reported by CityAM in July 2025 (based on Norton's own statutory accounts for the year to 31 March 2025), put **cumulative shareholder equity injected at £157.3 million** as of March 2025, with "almost £160m" pumped in overall including the original acquisition cost. **CALCULATION** — the gap between these two figures is **≈£90–95 million (≈55–60%) in a single 12-month window (FY26)**, which would represent a sharp acceleration in cash committed to a business that has never been profitable. This specific magnitude was **not identified or quantified in the Financials report** (which flagged Norton investment as ">₹1,200 cr+, likely higher by FY26" without a precise figure) and is a genuine open question for this department to escalate (Section 7 and Section 17 below).
5. **FACT (independently verified against primary UK filings)** — Norton Motorcycles' GBP-denominated pre-tax losses per its own Companies House accounts were approximately: FY21 ≈£10.2m, FY22 ≈£14.8m, FY23 ≈£29.7m, FY24 ≈£38.2m, **FY25 ≈£36.7m (a slight decrease from FY24)**. **CALCULATION/INFERENCE** — the Financials report states Norton's INR losses "rose in every disclosed year through FY26," but the independently-sourced GBP figures show FY25's loss was *lower* than FY24's in local-currency terms. Cross-checking with RBI's published GBP/INR annual averages (rising from the FY24 to FY26 window), this is best explained by an **FX-translation effect**: a weakening rupee against sterling makes a flat-or-falling GBP loss look larger when translated and consolidated into INR. This is a genuinely important nuance the Financials report did not surface: **part of the "worsening Norton losses" narrative in rupee terms is a currency-translation artifact, not entirely a deteriorating underlying UK operating loss** in FY25.
6. **FACT** — On 5 August 2026, the boards of TVS Credit Services Limited (TVS Motor's ~85%-owned NBFC subsidiary), Home Credit India Finance Private Limited (owned by promoter-controlled TVS Holdings/TVS Venu entities), STPL Trading and Services, and TVS Housing Finance approved a **composite scheme of amalgamation** merging all four into TVS Credit Services, expected to complete by May 2027. **This is a material forthcoming consolidation-scope change that post-dates and was not captured by the Financials report.** Once effective, Home Credit India's loan book, customer base (New-to-Credit, consumer-durables, personal-loan segments — a materially different, generally higher-risk credit profile than TVS Credit's core two-wheeler financing) and any associated asset-quality issues will flow into TVS Motor's own consolidated financial statements for the first time. TVS Motor is expected to retain majority control of the enlarged entity; TVS Holdings becomes a minority shareholder (per Crisil Ratings, 14 August 2026).
7. **FACT (independent primary source)** — CARE Ratings' own rationale (June 2025 reaffirmation) states TVS Motor's "**overall investment in equity instruments stood at ~86% (PY: 88%) of TVSM's reported net worth**" as of 31 March 2024, and explicitly flags "**significant exposure to its subsidiaries, a few of which are loss-making**" as a rating constraint. This independently corroborates — from a source that assigns real credit ratings with money behind them, not merely an aggregator — the Financials report's own finding (Section 6) that TVS Motor's ballooning "Investments" balance-sheet line is a genuine, third-party-recognised capital-allocation risk, not merely an internally-generated observation.
8. **NEW FINDING (governance/disclosure quality, not covered by Financials report)** — TVS Motor's own FY26 Annual Report discloses that **CMD Sudarshan Venu's total remuneration rose 46–48% YoY to ₹64.22 crore** (₹9.87 cr salary + ₹54.35 cr commission), while **overall managerial remuneration rose 44% and the median employee's remuneration rose only 6%** in a year when standalone PAT grew 37%. Executive pay grew faster than both profit growth and ordinary employee pay growth. This is a legitimate governance/pay-equity disclosure-quality observation for a company also disclosing strong performance-linked-pay philosophy; it is disclosed transparently (a **FACT**, not an inference), but the widening gap is a **JUDGMENT**-level item worth flagging to the Governance specialist.
9. **UNRESOLVED (confirmed, not resolved, by this department)** — The exact composition of consolidated contingent liabilities (₹717 cr FY22 → **₹1,490 cr FY26**, more than doubled) was **not locatable** in any secondary source reviewed; TVS Motor's own published Notes to Accounts extract confirms only the accounting *policy* for contingent liabilities and warranty provisions, not the FY26 itemised amounts (litigation vs. guarantees vs. tax disputes vs. capital commitments). This gap, already flagged by the Financials report, is independently confirmed as a genuine information gap in public secondary sources — a governance/legal specialist with access to the complete Notes to Accounts PDF should resolve it.
10. **CONTRADICTION IDENTIFIED (Section 15)** — Cross-referencing an independent aggregator snapshot obtained in this review shows FY24 consolidated Net Sales of **₹39,145 cr**, agreeing with the Pocketful.in figure the Financials report cited, and *not* the Screener.in/MarketsMojo figure of ₹38,778.82 cr that the Financials report also cited as in "full agreement" across sources. This department's independent check therefore **does not confirm** the Financials report's claim of three-way aggregator agreement on FY24 consolidated revenue; the ₹366 cr (~1%) discrepancy the Financials report flagged as immaterial-but-unresolved is corroborated as genuinely unresolved, with a slight independent lean toward ₹39,145 cr.

## 3. Accounting Policies

TVS Motor prepares standalone and consolidated financial statements under **Ind AS** (Indian Accounting Standards), consistent with all Indian listed companies since FY2016-17. Based on the Notes to Accounts extracts reviewed (**FACT**, from the published "Significant Accounting Policies" note):

- **Provisions:** recorded when there is a present legal or constructive obligation from past events, an outflow of resources is probable, and the amount can be reliably estimated; measured at the present value of management's best estimate using a pre-tax discount rate.
- **Contingent liabilities:** recognised per the standard Ind AS 37 framework (possible obligations depending on uncertain future events, or present obligations where outflow is not probable or not reliably measurable). **Notably, the accounting policy explicitly states: "Show cause notices are not considered as Contingent Liabilities unless converted into demand."** This is a standard, Ind-AS-compliant policy choice, but it is worth flagging as a **JUDGMENT-relevant fact**: a company facing multiple regulatory show-cause notices could have real exposure that does not appear anywhere in the contingent-liability note until a formal demand is issued. No evidence was found in this review of TVS Motor having outstanding show-cause notices of a scale that would matter, but the *policy itself* creates a structural reporting lag that any specialist relying purely on the contingent-liabilities note should be aware of.
- **Warranty provisions:** "Provision is made for estimated warranty claims in respect of vehicles sold which are still under warranty at the end of the reporting period. These claims are expected to be settled from the next financial year. Management estimates the pro[vision]..." (text truncated in the source extract obtained). **UNRESOLVED** — the specific estimation methodology (e.g., historical claim-rate percentage, per-unit accrual) and the FY26 warranty provision balance/movement were **not locatable** in the secondary sources reviewed. Given that TVS Motor's own product warranties run 3–5 years (per its published consumer warranty policy) and EV/battery warranty exposure is a live industry issue (a Mint article dated August 2025, reviewed in this research, reports that "TVS Motor, Bajaj Auto and Hero MotoCorp" are all "setting aside significantly larger sums to cover their warranty costs" specifically because "electric mobility is a relatively new technology... more likely to carry defects"), this is flagged as a **material unresolved question** (Section 17) — EV warranty-provision adequacy is a genuine accounting-quality question that neither this report nor the Financials report has been able to answer from the sources available.
- **Government grants (PLI):** not fully quoted in the sources obtained, but the recurring Key Audit Matter (Section 6 below) confirms this is treated as a judgment-heavy recognition area, tied to satisfying scheme conditions (localisation/value-addition thresholds under the PLI-Auto scheme).

No evidence was found of any **change in accounting policy** in FY24–FY26 other than the mechanical, NCLT-sanctioned restatement flowing from the Sundaram Auto Components amalgamation (Section 13 below), which is a business-combination/common-control accounting matter, not a change in accounting policy per se.

## 4. Revenue Recognition

TVS Motor's core revenue stream — sale of two- and three-wheelers and spare parts — is a **point-in-time recognition model** (delivery of vehicles to dealers), consistent with Ind AS 115 and standard automotive-industry practice; no unusual multi-element or over-time recognition method (e.g., percentage-of-completion) applies to the vehicle business. **INFERENCE** — this is a structurally simple, low-judgment revenue model relative to, say, a software or construction company, and is consistent with the Financials report's overall assessment of standalone earnings quality being good.

**The genuinely judgment-heavy area, as independently confirmed by the recurring auditor Key Audit Matter, is not "sales" revenue recognition but Government Grant (PLI scheme) income recognition** — this affects EBITDA/other-income presentation rather than core sales revenue timing, and is the same item the Financials report flagged (Section 10) as the source of the Q4 FY25 margin distortion. The auditor's own KAM description (**FACT**, quoted from the FY26 standalone Independent Auditors' Report): *"Government has announced various Grants to manufacturers of automobiles. The company in turn is availing the said grants on fulfilling the conditions attached to that. The recognition of Government grants is considered to be [a] key audit matter because of s[ignificant judgment involved]..."* (source text truncated before the full rationale, but the heading and first sentence are directly quoted from the primary document).

**Channel/dealer inventory:** No evidence was found — in either primary filings or secondary commentary — of TVS Motor engaging in aggressive "channel stuffing" (recognising revenue on units pushed into dealer inventory beyond genuine retail demand). The Financials report's own market-share analysis (retail FADA data vs. wholesale/SIAM data showing directionally consistent, not divergent, share gains) is *indirect* supporting evidence against channel stuffing, since a company inflating wholesale dispatches without matching retail pull-through would typically show a widening wholesale-vs-retail share gap over time; the two bases here move together. This is an **INFERENCE**, not a directly confirmed fact, since dealer-inventory-day data was not independently located.

**Recurring vs. non-recurring revenue mix:** consistent with the Financials report — vehicle sales are transactional, but spares/aftermarket and TVS Credit's financing-attach revenue provide a structurally recurring component. No new contrary evidence found.

## 5. Balance-Sheet Accounting

### Investments (the single largest and most consequential balance-sheet judgment area)
Standalone "Investments" grew from ₹3,315 cr (FY21) to ₹11,647 cr (FY26) — a 3.5x increase (per the Financials report, corroborated by the CNBC-TV18/goodreturns balance-sheet extract obtained independently in this research, which shows Non-Current Investments of ₹11,416.43 cr at FY26 plus current investments, broadly reconciling to the same order of magnitude). **FACT, independently corroborated (CARE Ratings, 23 June 2025 rationale):** "Overall investment in equity instruments stood at ~86% (PY: 88%) of TVSM's reported net worth as on March 31, 2024." **This is the single most important accounting-quality data point in this report**, because it comes from a rating agency whose job is specifically to independently verify balance-sheet risk, not from a data aggregator. An investment pool equivalent to ~86–88% of net worth, concentrated in subsidiaries whose economics differ sharply from the parent (a profitable-but-capital-hungry NBFC; a persistently loss-making UK motorcycle brand; several small, unproven ventures), represents **real accounting-quality complexity**: standard consolidated ratios (ROCE, ROE, asset turnover) computed off blended balance-sheet capital will systematically misstate the return profile of any one of the underlying businesses, exactly as the Financials report identified in its ROCE-discrepancy analysis (Section 11 of that report).

### Goodwill and intangibles
Consolidated goodwill (~₹731 cr) and other intangibles (₹1,121 cr FY22 → ₹2,806 cr FY26) are concentrated, per the Financials report's inference, in EV-platform development costs and Norton-related brand/engineering intangibles. **No impairment charge has been recognised to date** in any source reviewed, despite Norton's six-year loss history. **JUDGMENT** — this is a genuine, unresolved accounting-quality question: under Ind AS 36, a cash-generating unit with a sustained history of losses ordinarily triggers at minimum an annual impairment *test* (even if the conclusion is "no impairment required," citing the discounted future cash flows expected from the FY26-27 Norton product relaunch). Neither this report nor the Financials report found direct evidence — in the Notes to Accounts extracts available — of the specific impairment-testing disclosure, discount rate, or value-in-use assumptions used for Norton's carrying value. Given that TVS Motor's own AR states >£250m has now been committed to Norton, and the brand has generated cumulative losses exceeding its original enterprise value many times over, **the absence of a disclosed, detailed impairment-test rationale in the sources reviewed is flagged as a material unresolved question** (Section 17) for a specialist with direct access to the complete consolidated Notes to Accounts.

### Receivables and working capital
Standalone trade receivables jumped from ₹1,280 cr (FY25) to ₹2,087 cr (FY26), a 63% increase against 30% revenue growth (Financials report, Section 8, independently corroborated by the CNBC-TV18/goodreturns balance-sheet series). **INFERENCE** — while absolute debtor days remain low for the industry, a receivables growth rate more than double the revenue growth rate in a single year is the kind of divergence that, if it continued for two or more additional years, would warrant escalated forensic scrutiny (a classic early-warning signal in accounting-quality frameworks, e.g., Beneish-model-style receivables-growth tests). One year of divergence, on its own, is **not yet a red flag**, but this department flags it as the highest-priority balance-sheet metric to re-check when FY27 results are published.

### Inventory
Inventory grew slower than revenue in FY26 (Financials report) — a positive working-capital signal, independently consistent with the negative cash-conversion-cycle finding.

The ≈£90-95m gap over a single year is large enough that it should be explicitly reconciled — either (a) TVS Motor injected an unusually large tranche of capital into Norton specifically during FY26 (plausible, given the AR states the Solihull workforce grew 25% during 2025 ahead of the "Resurgence" product launch, and includes new UK manufacturing infrastructure spend), or (b) the £250m figure in the AR includes non-equity forms of support (working-capital funding via other Group entities, capitalised R&D spend recognised at the Group level rather than injected as Norton equity, or spend routed through TVS Motor (Singapore) Pte Limited rather than directly into Norton's own UK books) that would not appear in Norton's standalone UK equity-injection disclosure. **This department cannot resolve which explanation is correct from the sources available and flags it explicitly as an unresolved question for the CIO** (Section 17).

**Governance signal (independently sourced, not in the Financials report):** Norton's UK board saw a leadership change — Ralf Speth (former Jaguar Land Rover CEO, TVS Motor Group Chairman) was appointed a Norton director in March 2022, and Richard Arnold (former Manchester United CEO) joined the Norton board in June 2024 as part of a push toward "global expansion." **INFERENCE** — bringing in high-profile, expensive non-executive talent from unrelated industries (football club administration) to a persistently loss-making motorcycle subsidiary is consistent with the Financials report's own observation (Section 15/18) that management's continued "next year will be the turnaround" framing deserves scrutiny; it does not, on its own, prove anything either way about Norton's prospects.

**Product launch timeline cross-check (FACT):** TVS's FY26 AR states new Norton models (Manx, Manx R, Atlas, Atlas GT) are slated to enter the market in FY2026-27 — consistent with, but not yet evidence against, the multi-year pattern of "next year" turnaround signalling the Financials report documented (2024 CFO quote: "once the product line-up becomes available... there will be considerable improvement in profitability"). **This department confirms the pattern is now a minimum of three consecutive annual cycles** (FY24 commentary → FY25 AR → FY26 AR, all pointing to a subsequent year as the inflection point), reinforcing rather than resolving the Financials report's skepticism.

## 8. TVS Credit Services Review

**Scale and profitability (FACT, primary press release, 12 May 2026):** FY26 Total Income ₹7,196 cr (+9% YoY), PBT ₹1,238 cr (+21% YoY), **PAT ₹913 cr (+19% YoY, the highest-ever profit reported by the company)**, AUM ₹30,639 cr (+15% YoY vs ₹26,647 cr), customer base ~2.44 crore. These figures match the Financials report's citations closely (it cites AUM growth to "₹30,639 cr" and PAT contribution of "~₹913 cr" — fully corroborated).

**Independent leverage/capital-quality metrics not in the Financials report (FACT, ICRA rating rationale, 21 July 2026):**
- TVS Credit's **managed gearing improved to 4.6x as of March 2026, from 5.0x (March 2025) and 5.9x (March 2024)** — i.e., leverage has been falling for two consecutive years even as AUM grew.
- **Tier I capital improved to 16.5% (March 2026) from 12.8% (March 2024)**, reflecting "regular equity infusions in the past, including from TVSM Group."
- **Net profitability (PAT/Average Managed Assets) improved to 2.7% (FY26) from 2.5% (FY25) and 2.2% (FY24)** — a genuinely improving trend in the metric NBFC analysts consider most comparable across lenders of different size.

## 18. Department Conclusion

**Can an investor reasonably trust TVS Motor's reported financial statements and disclosures?**

> **Generally trustworthy, with some concerns concentrated in a small number of specific, identifiable areas.**

**Why "Generally trustworthy" rather than "Strongly trustworthy":** The core accounting infrastructure is sound. Statutory audit opinions have been clean and unqualified for at least four consecutive years across both TVS Motor and TVS Credit Services; the one statutory-auditor rotation identified in this research was routine and Companies-Act-compliant, not dispute-driven; related-party transactions are extensively disclosed, board-approved unanimously, and priced on a documented arm's-length basis; the two most significant recent structural accounting events (the Sundaram Auto Components merger and the NCRPS bonus-preference-share scheme) were both NCLT-sanctioned, transparently disclosed, and independently verifiable via primary court and exchange filings; and management has, on at least one occasion (the Q4 FY25 PLI "normalisation"), proactively disclosed and explained a distortion in its own headline numbers rather than letting it stand unexplained. No evidence of manipulation, undisclosed related-party abuse, aggressive revenue recognition, or channel stuffing was found by either this department or the Financials specialist.

**Why "with some concerns" rather than "Strongly trustworthy":** Three specific, named gaps prevent a stronger rating. First, this department identified a **quantifiable, currently unreconciled discrepancy of roughly £90-95 million between TVS Motor's own Annual Report claim of cumulative Norton investment and Norton's own UK statutory accounts** — a genuine disclosure-consistency question, not yet resolved by any source available. Second, **no visible impairment-testing disclosure exists for Norton's carrying value despite six years of continuous losses**, which is the kind of gap that, left unaddressed, erodes confidence in balance-sheet carrying values generally. Third, the **composition of a rapidly-growing (near-doubled) consolidated contingent-liability balance remains completely opaque** in every public source this department could access. None of these three items is evidence of dishonesty or fraud — they are disclosure-completeness gaps, most likely resolvable by a reader with direct access to the full Notes to Accounts (which this department, working from secondary and excerpted primary sources, could not fully obtain) — but until resolved, they justify "some concerns" rather than an unqualified "strongly trustworthy" rating. The standalone automotive business's financial statements can be relied upon with high confidence; the consolidated group's statements can be relied upon with good-but-not-complete confidence, specifically because of Norton, contingent liabilities, and (going forward) the incoming Home Credit India consolidation.
