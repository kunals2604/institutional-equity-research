# Filings & Accounting Research — TVS Motor Company Limited

## 1. Executive Findings
- **The Core Duality**: TVS Motor Company Limited exhibits a sharp structural dichotomy between its standalone automobile manufacturing operations and its consolidated perimeter `[FACT]`. Standalone operations are financially conservative, highly cash-generative, and operate on near-zero/negative working capital (`[CALCULATION]` ~2 days of sales), generating `[FACT]` ₹3,617.00 Cr of operating cash flow against `[FACT]` ₹2,083.00 Cr of Profit After Tax (PAT) in FY24 (CFO/PAT ratio of `[CALCULATION]` 1.74x) with minimal debt (`[FACT]` Standalone Debt/Equity: 0.20x).
- **Consolidated Leverage Distortion**: Consolidated debt stands at `[FACT]` ₹25,287.00 Cr (Consolidated D/E of `[CALCULATION]` >3.1x), which does not reflect automobile manufacturing indebtedness. Over `[CALCULATION]` 94% of consolidated borrowings reside entirely within `[FACT]` TVS Credit Services Limited, a fast-growing captive Non-Banking Financial Company (NBFC) with Assets Under Management (AUM) of `[FACT]` ₹25,900.00 Cr.
- **Overseas Capital Drag & Negative Subsidiary Drag**: Despite TVS Credit Services generating `[FACT]` ₹572.00 Cr in PAT in FY24 (TVS Motor's 80.69% share = `[CALCULATION]` ₹461.55 Cr), Consolidated PAT was only `[FACT]` ₹1,754.00 Cr compared to Standalone PAT of `[FACT]` ₹2,083.00 Cr. This reveals a massive `[CALCULATION]` ~₹790.55 Cr annual net profit drag from overseas subsidiaries—primarily `[FACT]` The Norton Motorcycle Co. Ltd (UK) and `[FACT]` Swiss E-Mobility Group (SEMG Holding, Switzerland). TVS Motor has deployed over `[FACT]` ₹1,000.00 Cr into Norton since acquisition and USD 100M+ into SEMG, absorbing parent capital without operational breakeven.
- **Balance Sheet Concentration in Subsidiaries**: As of March 31, 2024, `[FACT]` ₹6,828.07 Cr of Standalone Net Worth (`[FACT]` ₹7,731.04 Cr) is invested in Non-Current Investments (predominantly subsidiaries and associates), representing `[CALCULATION]` **88.32%** of total equity. TVS Motor's standalone balance sheet operates essentially as an automotive factory fused to a massive investment holding company.
- **Auditor Governance Profile**: Audited for over 40 years by `[FACT]` M/s Sundaram & Srinivasan, Chartered Accountants (ICAI FRN: 004207S), a Chennai-based firm founded in 1943 with deep historical ties to the TVS founding family `[FACT]`. Despite TVS Motor's ₹38,000+ Cr consolidated revenue, multi-country footprint, and ₹1.1+ lakh crore market capitalization, the company has resisted appointing an international Big-4 audit firm `[JUDGMENT]`.
- **Managerial Remuneration Escalation**: Managing Director Sudarshan Venu's remuneration reached `[FACT]` ₹64.22 Cr in FY26 (comprising `[FACT]` ₹9.87 Cr salary/perquisites and `[FACT]` ₹54.35 Cr profit-linked commission), representing `[FACT]` 436 times the median employee remuneration, making him one of India's highest-compensated automotive executives and drawing proxy advisory scrutiny `[FACT]`.
- **Department Trustworthiness Classification**: **Generally trustworthy** `[JUDGMENT]`. Reported domestic operational figures are verified, conservative, and backed by robust cash collections; however, analytical adjustments are strictly required for captive NBFC debt de-consolidation, overseas subsidiary operating drains, and promoter remuneration escalation.

## 3. Accounting Policies
- **Revenue Recognition (Ind AS 115)**: Revenue from sale of vehicles, spare parts, and accessories is recognized at a point in time when control of the goods is transferred to the customer (dealers or overseas distributors) `[FACT]`. Control is transferred upon dispatch/delivery depending on shipping terms (Ex-factory for domestic dealers, FOB/CIF for export consignments) `[FACT]`. Trade discounts, volume rebates, cash incentives, and performance allowances are estimated using the expected value method and netted directly against reported revenue `[FACT]`.
- **Property, Plant and Equipment & Depreciation (Ind AS 16)**: PPE is carried at historical cost less accumulated depreciation and impairment `[FACT]`. Depreciation is computed on a Straight-Line Method (SLM) based on estimated useful lives specified in Schedule II of the Companies Act, 2013, except where management's technical evaluation justifies different useful lives `[FACT]`:
  - Specialized Tooling, Dies, and Moulds: 3 to 8 years `[FACT]`.
  - Plant and Machinery: 15 years `[FACT]`.
  - Buildings: 30 to 60 years `[FACT]`.
  - Computer Software and Systems: 3 to 5 years `[FACT]`.
- **Inventory Valuation (Ind AS 2)**: Stated at the lower of cost and net realizable value `[FACT]`. Cost of raw materials, components, and consumables is determined on a Weighted Average basis `[FACT]`. Finished goods and work-in-progress include direct materials, direct labor, and an appropriate allocation of fixed and variable manufacturing overheads based on normal operating capacity `[FACT]`.
- **Leases (Ind AS 116)**: Right-of-Use (ROU) assets and corresponding lease liabilities recognized on balance sheet for all operating leases (depots, branch offices, logistics hubs), except short-term leases (<12 months) and low-value assets `[FACT]`.
- **Financial Instruments (Ind AS 109)**: Financial assets classified as amortized cost, Fair Value through Other Comprehensive Income (FVTOCI), or Fair Value through Profit and Loss (FVTPL) `[FACT]`. In Standalone financial statements, investments in equity shares of subsidiaries, joint ventures, and associates are carried at cost less impairment, as permitted under Ind AS 27 `[FACT]`.
