# Moat and Competitive-Dynamics Report: Bharti Airtel Limited (BHARTIARTL)

## Mandate, Scope and Independence

**Decision questions answered.** (1) Has any Airtel advantage produced returns above the cost of capital, or better pricing, retention or share than competitors, over 10 years? (2) What is the mechanism of each claimed advantage, the evidence for and against it, and why can rivals not copy it? (3) How did a capable, well-capitalised attacker (Reliance Jio, 2016) attack the position, and what happened? (4) Is the advantage structural, or a product of the tariff cycle, management or temporary conditions? (5) Is it widening, stable or eroding, and what competitive-advantage period (CAP) does the evidence support?

**Scope.** Moat mechanisms were tested segment by segment as the brief requires: India mobile (the economic core), Homes (fixed and fixed-wireless broadband), Airtel Business (enterprise), Digital TV (DTH), passive infrastructure (Indus Towers, a subsidiary since 19-Nov-2024) and Africa. Data centres (Nxtra) are not reported as a separate segment and could not be tested. The canonical ROCE/ROIC series belongs to financials and market-share history to industry. I retrieved the primary figures independently (company-reported ROCE, segment notes, TRAI reports) and list the dependencies in the questions section.

**Sector playbooks applied.** `methodology/SECTOR_PLAYBOOKS.md` §16 Telecom and media (primary) and §18 Holding companies (listed-subsidiary structure). Lenses owned: T1 (competitive side), T4 (inversion on the moat), T12 (competitive-advantage period).

## Key Findings

1. **The 10-year return record does not show a moat.** Consolidated pre-tax ROCE beat a pre-tax hurdle of 14.7% (11% WACC) in only 3 of the last 10 fiscal years (FY2024-FY2026) and 3 of 14 since FY2013. The median post-tax spread over FY2017-FY2026 is about -3.4 pp, and between -2.4 and -4.4 pp across a 10-12% WACC range [CALCULATION: MOAT-C007; ESTIMATE: MOAT-C006]. ROCE reached 19.0% in FY2026 [FACT: MOAT-C001], up from a 2.53% trough in FY2019 [FACT: MOAT-C004].
2. **The deepest-pocketed attacker breached the position once, at large cost.** After Jio's 2016 launch, India mobile revenue fell 25.9% (FY2016-FY2019) and the segment result swung by INR -185,082 Mn to a loss [CALCULATION: MOAT-C014]. ARPU fell 48.5% to Rs 100 [CALCULATION: MOAT-C022; FACT: MOAT-C018], and at the trough Airtel's ARPU was about 2.5% *below* Jio's [CALCULATION: MOAT-C026]. Airtel survived, but its excess returns disappeared for about six years.
3. **What protects Airtel today is mainly industry structure, not firm-specific advantage.** The barrier is the capital and time needed to build a national spectrum-plus-network footprint: of the order of INR 2.7-2.9 lakh crore and several years [ESTIMATE: MOAT-C060]. This holds in a market that consolidated to three private players and one state operator [MANAGEMENT STATEMENT: MOAT-C047; MOAT-C048]. Two of the three private players (Jio and Airtel) now take about 85% of access revenue [CALCULATION: MOAT-C033].
4. **There is a firm-specific edge, but a modest one.** Airtel's ARPU is 20.1% above Jio's (Q4FY2026) [CALCULATION: MOAT-C024], and its share of access revenue (about 40%) runs above its subscriber share [CALCULATION: MOAT-C033; FACT: MOAT-C029]. This reflects a premium customer mix (postpaid, smartphone, roaming). It does **not** reflect lock-in: Airtel's churn of 2.4% a month is higher than Jio's 1.7% [FACT: MOAT-C019; MOAT-C023].
5. **Pricing power is not demonstrated over the decade.** ARPU rose 32.5% from Q4FY2016 to Q4FY2026 (2.85% a year), while CPI rose 51-59%, so real ARPU *fell* 12-17% [CALCULATION: MOAT-C020]. Since 2019 the gains came from industry-wide tariff repairs. Management says returns remain inadequate without further tariff repair and that the industry needs a minimum ARPU of Rs 300 [MANAGEMENT STATEMENT: MOAT-C052; MOAT-C054; MOAT-C062].
6. **Returns in India mobile are recovering but still thin on the capital deployed.** The segment result was 12.8% of average segment assets in FY2026, against 9.4% in FY2016 and -3.5% in FY2019 [CALCULATION: MOAT-C012]. The incremental return since FY2021 is high (about 46%) because tariff repair flowed through an existing asset base [CALCULATION: MOAT-C042].
7. **The segment moats differ.** Airtel Business earned 18-25% pre-tax on segment assets in every year FY2019-FY2026, including the price-war years: the most durable evidence of an advantage [CALCULATION: MOAT-C040]. Africa rose from about 1% to 15-17% [CALCULATION: MOAT-C041]. Homes is growing fast, but returns are falling and Jio leads in fixed and fixed-wireless broadband [FACT: MOAT-C037; MOAT-C063; MOAT-C023]. DTH returns fell from 32% to -3% [CALCULATION: MOAT-C039].
8. **Verdict.** The moat is **Narrow** and the trajectory **Stable**. The primary mechanism is an entry barrier from scale, spectrum and capital in a consolidated oligopoly, plus a premium-mix position. The evidence supports a CAP of **5-10 years**, contingent on rational pricing by Jio [JUDGMENT].

## Moat Mechanism Tests

Each test answers six questions: (1) what the advantage is; (2) its economic channel; (3) the observable evidence if it were real, and whether that evidence is present; (4) why a competitor cannot reproduce it, with cost and time stated as an ESTIMATE; (5) what could erode it in 5-10 years; (6) how a capable, well-capitalised competitor would attack it and what history shows.

### A2 + A3: Entry barrier from scale, spectrum and capital in a consolidated oligopoly (India mobile), the primary candidate mechanism
1. **What.** A new national entrant must buy spectrum in auctions, build or lease a national 4G/5G network, fibre backhaul and distribution, and then absorb years of losses while it acquires customers. With three private players and BSNL, incumbents can earn more than in a fragmented market, provided they price rationally [MANAGEMENT STATEMENT: MOAT-C048].
2. **Channel.** Price (industry discipline) and capital intensity. Fixed costs spread over a 373 Mn customer base [FACT: MOAT-C019].
3. **Evidence present?** *Partly.* The industry consolidated (Vodafone and Idea merged; others exited) [MANAGEMENT STATEMENT: MOAT-C047; MOAT-C048]. The top two private operators take about 85% of access AGR [CALCULATION: MOAT-C033], and no new mobile entrant has appeared since 2016. But the barrier **did not stop** an entrant with Reliance's balance sheet. When that entrant came in, it removed Airtel's returns instead of being deterred [CALCULATION: MOAT-C014; MOAT-C012].
4. **Why hard to copy.** Replicating Airtel's India mobile asset base would take of the order of INR 2.7-2.9 lakh crore of capital, before ramp-up losses [ESTIMATE: MOAT-C060]. On Jio's precedent, reaching material share would take years: 9.3% of wireless subscribers about 7 months after launch and 26.4% within about 2.5 years [FACT: MOAT-C027; MOAT-C028]. This barrier excludes almost every potential entrant except a conglomerate or a state-backed operator.
5. **Erosion paths.** A renewed price war by Jio. The barrier is lower in specific segments, such as satellite broadband in rural areas and fixed-wireless access. Policy changes that lower spectrum cost for new entrants or revive Vodafone Idea with state support [FACT: MOAT-C056; MOAT-C059].
6. **Attack history.** Jio, 2016-2019, described in the Competitor Attack Test. Outcome: the attacker won about 26% subscriber share in 2.5 years, the incumbents' returns collapsed, and weaker incumbents exited or merged. **Verdict on this mechanism:** it is real as a barrier to *most* entrants, and it explains why returns could recover after FY2020. It is a moat that the *industry* shares (with Jio and a weakened Vodafone Idea), not one specific to Airtel [INFERENCE: MOAT-C033; MOAT-C014].

### A1: Premium customer mix and "industry leading ARPU" (India mobile)
1. **What.** A higher share of postpaid, smartphone and high-usage customers, and a deliberate focus on "quality customers", which produce a higher revenue per user than rivals.
2. **Channel.** Price/mix, which shows up as revenue share above subscriber share.
3. **Evidence present?** *Yes since about FY2020, no before.* Airtel's ARPU is 20.1% above Jio's in Q4FY2026 and 18.8% above in Q4FY2025 [CALCULATION: MOAT-C024]. Bharti takes about 40% of top-4 access AGR [CALCULATION: MOAT-C033] against a comparable wireless subscriber share of 33.7% in Mar-2025 [FACT: MOAT-C029]. But at the Q4FY2019 trough Airtel's ARPU was about 2.5% *below* Jio's [CALCULATION: MOAT-C026], and in Q3FY2019 Airtel deliberately shed 48.5 Mn low-value customers [FACT: MOAT-C018]. The premium is therefore partly a result of pruning the customer base and of tariff repair, not something that protected Airtel during the attack.
4. **Why hard to copy.** Jio could pursue the same premium mix at modest cost. The obstacles are more about Jio's *choice* (it optimises for volume and its ecosystem) and the habits of postpaid customers than about a cost barrier [INFERENCE]. Copy time: ESTIMATE 2-4 years if Jio chose to target the premium segment. The basis is that Jio already leads in 5G and fixed wireless [FACT: MOAT-C023]. Low confidence.
5. **Erosion.** Jio or Vodafone Idea target postpaid and roaming customers. Unlimited 5G offered free compresses the price ladder [MANAGEMENT STATEMENT: MOAT-C044].
6. **Attack.** No direct attack on the premium segment is documented. Verdict: a real but modest positioning advantage. It depends on management and is not a structural lock-in [JUDGMENT].

### A4: Brand, distribution and experience (switching costs: choice vs lock-in)
1. **What.** Brand trust, retail reach, digital self-service, spam and fraud protection.
2. **Channel.** Retention.
3. **Evidence present?** *No measurable switching cost.* Airtel's monthly churn averaged 2.95% over 25 disclosed quarters [CALCULATION: MOAT-C035] and is 2.4% now, against Jio's 1.7% [FACT: MOAT-C019; MOAT-C023]. That implies an average customer life of about 42 months at Airtel and 59 months at Jio [CALCULATION: MOAT-C035]. Over 92% of the base is prepaid [FACT: MOAT-C019]: customers re-choose each recharge cycle. Retention is choice, not lock-in.
4. **Copyability.** Spam and fraud tools and digital journeys can be copied in months. Brand perception takes years, but it showed no ability to defend price in FY2017-FY2019 [CALCULATION: MOAT-C022].
5-6. **Erosion and attack.** Any operator offering a lower price. In 2016-2019 Airtel kept its customers only by matching prices [MANAGEMENT STATEMENT: MOAT-C050]. Verdict: not a moat [JUDGMENT].

### A5: Convergence and home broadband (Homes)
1-2. **What and channel.** Fibre plus fixed-wireless access (FWA) home broadband bundled with mobile and content; the channels are retention and ARPU per home.
3. **Evidence present?** *Growth, but no advantage yet.* Revenue grew 31.7% to INR 77,747 Mn in FY2026, with customers at 14.2 Mn [FACT: MOAT-C037]. But the pre-tax return on segment assets fell from 18.1% (FY2024) to 8.7% (FY2026) as investment accelerated [CALCULATION: MOAT-C039]. Jio leads: about 13 Mn AirFiber (FWA) subscribers against Airtel's FWA base of over 3 Mn [FACT: MOAT-C023; MOAT-C037], and 30.45% of wired internet subscribers against about 22% for Bharti [FACT: MOAT-C063].
4-6. **Copyability, erosion, attack.** Fibre home passes and FWA are being built by Jio faster and at larger scale. Verdict: an **Emerging** position at best. The challenger with the deepest pockets is ahead [JUDGMENT].

### A6: Enterprise relationships and network assets (Airtel Business)
1-2. **What and channel.** Multi-year enterprise contracts, a national fibre and international cable footprint, a single point of contact; the channels are retention and price.
3. **Evidence present?** *Yes: the strongest return evidence in the group.* Pre-tax return on average segment assets was between 18.2% and 24.8% every year FY2019-FY2026, *including* the price-war years [CALCULATION: MOAT-C040], and 21.6% in FY2026 [CALCULATION: MOAT-C039]. Revenue rose from INR 96,993 Mn (FY2016) to INR 211,766 Mn (FY2026) [FACT: MOAT-C038; `calc/moat_segment_inputs.csv`].
4. **Copyability.** Enterprise switching costs (integration, multi-site contracts, managed services) are real but incomplete. Jio, Tata Communications and Vodafone Idea compete. ESTIMATE: 3-5 years for a rival to build comparable enterprise-grade fibre and sales coverage. The basis is the relative stability of the segment's revenue and returns through FY2017-FY2021 [CALCULATION: MOAT-C040]. Low confidence.
5-6. **Erosion and attack.** FY2026 segment revenue was lower than FY2025 (INR 211,766 Mn vs 220,935 Mn) [FACT: MOAT-C038; `calc/moat_segment_inputs.csv`], which needs explanation (pricing or portfolio pruning; not resolved here). Verdict: **Moderate** evidence of an advantage, but the segment is small relative to group revenue (INR 211,766 Mn of 2,109,728 Mn) [FACT: MOAT-C045] [JUDGMENT].

### A7: Tower scale (passive infrastructure / Indus)
The FY2026 pre-tax return on segment assets was 11.0% [CALCULATION: MOAT-C039], roughly at the cost of capital. Tower economics depend on tenancy, and Vodafone Idea, a weak tenant, is now state-supported [FACT: MOAT-C056]. Only one full year of consolidated data exists, so the mechanism was not tested in depth. Verdict: **Narrow** at most; insufficient evidence [JUDGMENT].

### A8: Africa scale and mobile money
Pre-tax return on segment assets (in INR) rose from 0.7% (FY2016) to 16.7% (FY2026) [CALCULATION: MOAT-C041]. Management cites a mobile-money base of 54.1 Mn [MANAGEMENT STATEMENT: MOAT-C064], and payments networks can carry genuine network effects. The rising returns are consistent with an emerging advantage. However, country-level market shares, the strength of MTN and Vodacom/M-Pesa, and currency effects were not tested from primary Airtel Africa data. Verdict: a **Narrow** advantage, with low confidence [JUDGMENT].

### A9: Balance sheet and staying power
Airtel stayed solvent through FY2017-FY2021 and absorbed a FY2020 loss of INR 321,832 Mn [FACT: MOAT-C043]. It then gained subscriber share as weaker rivals exited, although part of that share was acquired [CALCULATION: MOAT-C034]. Staying power is an advantage against weak rivals, not against Jio. Verdict: this is a source of *survival*, not of excess returns [JUDGMENT].

## Competitor Attack Test

**The documented offensive: Reliance Jio, launched September 2016.** Reliance Jio was the attacker with the deepest pockets in Indian telecom. Its Q4FY2026 run-rate shows FY2026 EBITDA of INR 76,255 crore and 524.4 Mn customers [FACT: MOAT-C023]. Management described the entry as "a new operator offering free voice and data" [MANAGEMENT STATEMENT: MOAT-C047], "price war by new entrant" [MANAGEMENT STATEMENT: MOAT-C049] and "services below cost" [MANAGEMENT STATEMENT: MOAT-C050].

| Test | Evidence | Outcome for Airtel |
|---|---|---|
| Did the attacker win share? | Jio went from absent (Mar-2016) to 9.29% (Mar-2017), 26.40% (Mar-2019) and 40.6% (Mar-2025) of wireless subscribers [FACT: MOAT-C027; MOAT-C028; MOAT-C029] | Airtel's own share dipped in FY2017, then rose, helped by acquisitions and rivals' exits [CALCULATION: MOAT-C034] |

**Reading.** The attack shows that Airtel's position did not protect its returns against a determined, better-capitalised entrant willing to price below cost. What the position *did* do was give Airtel the scale, spectrum and balance sheet to survive and to inherit share from the operators that did not. This is the profile of a durable **survivor in an oligopoly**, not of a franchise with pricing power [INFERENCE: MOAT-C014; MOAT-C034]. One failed attacker would not prove that all attacks fail, and the reverse also holds. The relevant lesson is that the *only* entrant able to breach the barrier was a conglomerate prepared to lose money for years.

**Current and prospective attackers.**
- *Jio (incumbent leader).* It is the rival best able to restart a price war. It now earns a 51.9% EBITDA margin and INR 30,053 crore of PAT [FACT: MOAT-C023], and it filed a DRHP for an IPO (fresh issue only; proceeds of up to INR 27,500 crore to repay debt) [FACT: MOAT-C058]. A listed Jio has public-market return incentives, which argues against near-term predatory pricing. That is an inference about incentives, not evidence [INFERENCE]. Jio is already attacking where Airtel is weaker: fixed-wireless access (about 13 Mn AirFiber subscribers) and wired broadband (30.45% share) [FACT: MOAT-C023; MOAT-C063].
- *Vodafone Idea, state-supported.* AGR relief and the conversion of dues to equity keep a third private player alive [FACT: MOAT-C056]. Its ARPU (about Rs 190-195) sits below Airtel's [FACT: MOAT-C057]. If Vodafone Idea survives, there will be no further market-share windfall from its exit. Its ability to fund a price war is limited.
- *BSNL 4G.* No measurable revenue effect yet: AGR fell 6.19% YoY, and BSNL holds 2.9% of top-4 access AGR [CALCULATION: MOAT-C061].
- *Satellite (Starlink, OneWeb, Jio-SES).* These operators are licensed, but by the cutoff they had no commercial launch and no final spectrum assignment [FACT: MOAT-C059]. Airtel has turned Starlink into a distribution partner in India and Africa rather than a rival [MANAGEMENT STATEMENT: MOAT-C055]. The threat is concentrated in rural and enterprise backhaul, not urban mobile [INFERENCE].

## Evidence Against the Moat

This evidence is given equal weight with the evidence for a moat, and it is substantial:
1. **Return duration fails.** Only 3 of 10 (and 3 of 14) years show ROCE above the hurdle [CALCULATION: MOAT-C007]. The pre-Jio period also shows sub-hurdle returns (about 7%) [FACT: MOAT-C003].
2. **Prices fell in real terms over the decade** [CALCULATION: MOAT-C020], and nominal gains came only from tariff rounds taken by the whole industry [MANAGEMENT STATEMENT: MOAT-C062].
3. **Margin compression during entry**: the India mobile EBITDA-margin proxy fell from 39.0% to 22.7%, and the segment ran a loss for two years [CALCULATION: MOAT-C015; FACT: MOAT-C011].
4. **No premium at the trough**: Airtel's ARPU was below Jio's in Q4FY2019 [CALCULATION: MOAT-C026].
5. **Churn is higher than Jio's** (2.4% vs 1.7%) [FACT: MOAT-C019; MOAT-C023]. Customers in a prepaid market are not locked in.
6. **Management's own words.** India ROCE was "low at 9.4 percent" (FY2024) and "only 11 percent" (FY2025) [MANAGEMENT STATEMENT: MOAT-C051; MOAT-C052]. Management says Rs 300 ARPU is the minimum needed for respectable returns, against Rs 257 actual [MANAGEMENT STATEMENT: MOAT-C062; FACT: MOAT-C019]. Its FY2026 threat list includes "Entry of disruptive players and heightened price competition" and "Low tariffs" [MANAGEMENT STATEMENT: MOAT-C044].
7. **Losing adjacent battles to the attacker with the deepest pockets**: Jio leads in FWA and wired broadband [FACT: MOAT-C023; MOAT-C063]. Homes returns fell to 8.7% [CALCULATION: MOAT-C039]. DTH returns turned negative [CALCULATION: MOAT-C039].
8. **Share gains were partly bought.** The Telenor India and Tata Teleservices consumer-mobile bases were acquired, so organic share gain is smaller than the headline +9.4 pp [CALCULATION: MOAT-C034].
9. **Regulatory support for a rival**: the state's equity and the dues freeze at Vodafone Idea prolong four-player competition [FACT: MOAT-C056].

## Moat versus Management and Cycle

**Moat versus management.** Two parts must be separated. (a) The *structural* barrier (spectrum, network scale, consolidated market) would survive average management, because it rests on sunk capital and market structure. (b) The *premium-mix* position (ARPU premium, revenue share above subscriber share) was a deliberate strategy of the 2013-2025 management team. It included the purge of low-value customers in FY2019 [FACT: MOAT-C018], and it would erode faster under weaker execution [INFERENCE]. The CEO transition on 1-Jan-2026 (Gopal Vittal to Executive Vice Chairman; Shashwat Sharma as India MD & CEO) [FACT: MOAT-C046] keeps the architect in the group. ARPU after the transition (Rs 259 in Q3FY2026, Rs 257 in Q4FY2026) shows no break yet [FACT: MOAT-C019].

**Moat versus cycle.** India telecom's "cycle" is a *pricing-behaviour* cycle, not a supply-demand cycle. The recovery in returns over FY2021-FY2026 coincides with industry tariff repairs and the exit of capacity (operators), not with a widening firm-specific advantage. That is why the incremental return looks high (about 46%) while capital intensity falls [CALCULATION: MOAT-C042]. Management itself makes further gains contingent on tariff repair [MANAGEMENT STATEMENT: MOAT-C054; MOAT-C053]. **Does the moat provide a floor in a downturn?** The FY2017-FY2020 evidence says only a weak one. India mobile went loss-making [FACT: MOAT-C011], and only the more resilient segments (Airtel Business at 18%+ [CALCULATION: MOAT-C040], and Africa improving [CALCULATION: MOAT-C041]) kept consolidated ROCE positive [FACT: MOAT-C004].

## Four-Layer Evidence Crosswalk

### Layer 1 — Bedrock Compounder
- **Relevant evidence:** 10- and 14-year return spread and duration; segment returns; pricing vs inflation; the attack test; the CAP.
- **Supports:** ROCE has been above the hurdle for three consecutive years, reaching 19.0% in FY2026 [FACT: MOAT-C001; CALCULATION: MOAT-C007]. India mobile incremental returns are high and capital intensity is falling [CALCULATION: MOAT-C042]. Airtel Business returns are durable [CALCULATION: MOAT-C040]. The consolidated market and high entry cost act as a barrier [ESTIMATE: MOAT-C060].
- **Weakens:** Only 3 of 10 years are above the hurdle, with a median post-tax spread of about -3.4 pp [CALCULATION: MOAT-C007]. Real ARPU fell [CALCULATION: MOAT-C020]. A single well-funded attacker erased returns for about six years [CALCULATION: MOAT-C014]. Churn shows no lock-in [CALCULATION: MOAT-C035]. Management says returns remain inadequate without more tariff repair [MANAGEMENT STATEMENT: MOAT-C062].
- **Domain implications:** The mechanism that could protect high incremental returns for a decade is industry structure plus disciplined pricing. It is not a firm-specific franchise, so a 10-year-plus compounding thesis rests on the behaviour of the other players.
- **Missing information:** Through-cycle ROIC on the METRIC_DEFINITIONS basis (financials); Jio's capital base and ROCE; a primary series for operator-wise revenue share before FY2025.
- **Plausibility effect:** Lowers — the moat evidence supports a CAP of 5-10 years, not the decade-plus protected compounding that Layer 1 requires, and the 10-year return record fails the spread-duration test.

### Layer 2 — High-Growth Business
- **Relevant evidence:** Whether advantages strengthen with scale; saturation; the adjacent growth businesses (Homes, enterprise, Africa).
- **Supports:** India mobile revenue CAGR was 7.26% over FY2016-FY2026 [CALCULATION: MOAT-C065]. Homes grew 31.7% in FY2026 [FACT: MOAT-C037]. Africa's returns are rising [CALCULATION: MOAT-C041].
- **Weakens:** Wireless market growth is "averaging about 6%" [MANAGEMENT STATEMENT: MOAT-C053], and 79.5% of mobile customers already use smartphones [FACT: MOAT-C036]. In Homes the scale advantage belongs to Jio [FACT: MOAT-C023; MOAT-C063]. Scale economies in mobile are shared by the duopoly rather than accruing to Airtel alone.
- **Domain implications:** Network scale economies exist but are not widening relative to Jio. Growth adjacencies face the attacker with the deepest pockets.
- **Missing information:** FWA and fibre unit economics by operator; Airtel Africa country-level share and mobile-money data.
- **Plausibility effect:** Lowers — the core market is saturating, and Airtel is the challenger, not the scale leader, in its main growth adjacency.

### Layer 3 — Cyclical / Event-Driven Opportunity
- **Relevant evidence:** Whether returns are driven by the moat or by the pricing cycle, and whether the moat sets a floor.
- **Supports:** Returns track industry pricing rounds and consolidation: ARPU fell 48.5% and then rose 157% [CALCULATION: MOAT-C022]. Management ties further upside to tariff repair [MANAGEMENT STATEMENT: MOAT-C054]. That is the profile of a pricing cycle, where a further repair is the event.
- **Weakens:** The current point in the cycle is late-recovery: ROCE is at a 14-year high [FACT: MOAT-C001], and the leading private operators hold stable shares [CALCULATION: MOAT-C033]. The floor in a downturn was weak: India mobile was loss-making in FY2019-FY2020 [FACT: MOAT-C011].
- **Domain implications:** Much of the FY2021-FY2026 return improvement is cyclical (tariff and structure) rather than moat-driven. Whether the next move is another repair or a renewed price war is the swing factor.
- **Missing information:** The timing and size of any next tariff round; Jio's pricing intentions after the IPO; the final settlement of Vodafone Idea's AGR dues.
- **Plausibility effect:** Raises — the evidence shows returns driven mainly by the pricing cycle and industry structure rather than by a firm-specific moat, which fits the Layer 3 lens. Valuation must judge whether the cycle is already priced.

### Layer 4 — Venture-Style / Optionality Bet
- **Relevant evidence:** Whether optional businesses (Homes/FWA, data centres, Africa mobile money, satellite partnerships, enterprise digital) have a defensible path to a moat.
- **Supports:** Africa mobile money is a network-effect business with 54.1 Mn customers [MANAGEMENT STATEMENT: MOAT-C064]. The Starlink partnerships turn a potential threat into distribution [MANAGEMENT STATEMENT: MOAT-C055].
- **Weakens:** Homes and FWA are being contested by Jio at larger scale [FACT: MOAT-C023; MOAT-C063]. Data centres could not be tested (no segment disclosure). Satellite access is easily copied: Jio and OneWeb hold the same licences [FACT: MOAT-C059].
- **Domain implications:** None of the options shows a demonstrated, defensible moat yet. The mobile-money option is the most plausible but is held through a listed subsidiary.
- **Missing information:** Nxtra financials; Airtel Money (Africa) unit economics and market shares; FWA retention data.
- **Plausibility effect:** Insufficient evidence — the moat domain cannot show that any option has a defensible path to a moat from the evidence retrieved.

## Domain Conclusion

**Answers to the decision questions.**
1. *Excess returns sustained over 10 years?* **No.** Consolidated ROCE cleared the hurdle in 3 of 10 years [CALCULATION: MOAT-C007]. Pricing failed to keep up with inflation [CALCULATION: MOAT-C020]. Retention is not superior to Jio's [CALCULATION: MOAT-C035]. Share gains came largely from rivals' failures and acquisitions [CALCULATION: MOAT-C034]. Airtel Business is the exception [CALCULATION: MOAT-C040].
2. *Mechanisms.* The primary mechanism is an entry barrier from spectrum, network scale and capital in a consolidated oligopoly [ESTIMATE: MOAT-C060]. It is shared with Jio and is effective against all but conglomerate or state-backed entrants. The secondary mechanism is a premium-mix ARPU position (+20% vs Jio) [CALCULATION: MOAT-C024], which a rival could copy if it chose to. Brand and experience do not show up as retention [FACT: MOAT-C019].
3. *Attack test.* Jio's 2016 offensive succeeded in taking share and erasing incumbent returns for about six years. Airtel survived and consolidated its position; weaker rivals did not [CALCULATION: MOAT-C014; MOAT-C007].
4. *Structural vs cyclical.* The barrier is structural. The current level of returns is mainly the result of the industry pricing cycle and consolidation, and management makes further gains contingent on tariff repair [MANAGEMENT STATEMENT: MOAT-C054; MOAT-C062].
5. *Trajectory and CAP.* The position is stable in revenue share and widening in ARPU premium. Positions in adjacencies (Homes, DTH) are weakening [CALCULATION: MOAT-C033; MOAT-C024; MOAT-C039]. Net trajectory: stable. CAP 5-10 years [JUDGMENT].

**Formal classifications.**
- **Moat assessment:** Narrow. India mobile is narrow (industry-structure barrier plus a modest premium-mix edge); Airtel Business moderate; Africa narrow (low confidence); passive infrastructure narrow (insufficient history); Homes emerging; DTH none [JUDGMENT].
- **Trajectory:** Stable [JUDGMENT].
- **Durability horizon / CAP:** 5-10 years, based on the barrier cost and time [ESTIMATE: MOAT-C060], three years of positive spread [CALCULATION: MOAT-C007], and the single return-destroying attack in the past decade [CALCULATION: MOAT-C014].
- **Primary mechanism:** An entry barrier from spectrum, network scale and capital in a consolidated three-private-player market, plus a premium-mix (ARPU) position. Its economic value depends on rational industry pricing.
