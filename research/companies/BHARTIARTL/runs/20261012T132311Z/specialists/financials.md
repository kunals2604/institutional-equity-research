# Financials — Bharti Airtel Limited (BHARTIARTL)

## 1. Mandate, Scope and Independence

**Decision questions answered:** (1) through-cycle and incremental returns on capital; (2) profit-to-cash conversion over 5 and 10 years; (3) normalised owner earnings vs. reported PAT; (4) worst-year behaviour and today's balance-sheet resilience, including DoT deferred liabilities, AGR dues, leases and subsidiary exposures; (5) whether capital allocation created value per share over 10+ years; (6) scaling economics, normalised vs. current earnings and funding needs, for the four-layer crosswalk.

**Scope:** consolidated P&L, balance sheet and cash flow FY2011–FY2026 (16 fiscal years), 12 quarters (Q2FY2024–Q1FY2027), segment economics (India Mobile, Africa, Indus/passive infrastructure and others), debt and liquidity, per-share compounding, capital allocation, owner earnings and forensic screens. **Out of scope** (flagged to owners): accounting-policy forensics and auditor matters (filings), integrity and incentives (management), moat verdicts (moat), valuation, cost of capital and market data (valuation).

**Sector playbooks applied:** `SECTOR_PLAYBOOKS.md` §16 Telecom and media (primary): pre/post Ind AS 116 EBITDA, DoT deferred spectrum and AGR liabilities as quasi-debt, capitalised spectrum, net debt including/excluding leases, Africa FX. §18 Holding companies: listed subsidiaries (Airtel Africa plc, Indus Towers, Bharti Hexacom) drive minority leakage, so attributable and consolidated figures are shown separately. There is no captive lender of material size in the consolidated statements, so the lender profile was not run (Airtel Payments Bank is outside the scope of these numbers; filings should confirm its accounting treatment).

**Blockers:** none that stopped completion. The FY2026 annual report is not in the local corpus. Its consolidated-statements section was downloaded from airtel.in and read in full (pp.424–499). ## 2. Key Findings

1. **The business has shifted from a weak earner to a strong one. The shift is recent, and it follows a severe trough.** ROCE fell from 11.0 % (FY2016) to 2.5 % (FY2019) and has since risen every year to 18.8 % (FY2026) [CALCULATION: FIN-C022]. At a constant 25.17 % tax rate, ROIC was 1.8 % at the FY2019 trough and 13.9 % in FY2026, after mostly 5–9 % between FY2012 and FY2018 [CALCULATION: FIN-C024]. Through-cycle (FY2012–FY2026) returns were therefore modest. The current level is the best in the 16-year record.
2. **Incremental returns on the last five years' capital are high.** NOPAT (25.17 % tax) rose by ₹38,161 crore on ₹1,36,312 crore of extra invested capital between FY2021 and FY2026, an incremental ROIC of 28.0 %. The figure is 23.0 % on a 3-year window and 17.0 % on a 10-year window [CALCULATION: FIN-C025]. The FY2025–FY2026 windows include the inorganic Indus consolidation.
3. **Cash conversion depends on how much of the cash burden is counted.** Cumulative CFO is 4.18× PAT over FY2022–FY2026 [CALCULATION: FIN-C030]. After spectrum, lease principal and interest, equity FCF was only 0.72× PAT over five years and 0.23× over ten [CALCULATION: FIN-C031]. FY2026 was the first year in which equity FCF (₹53,054 crore) far exceeded attributable PAT (₹26,695 crore) [CALCULATION: FIN-C032]. That was helped by near-zero spectrum cash after the FY2025 prepayments [FACT: FIN-C011; FIN-C004].
4. **Reported earnings are heavily distorted by one-offs. FY2025 EPS overstated underlying profit by about 90 %.** Underlying profit attributable (excluding exceptional items and one-off tax items) was ₹17,573 crore in FY2025, against ₹33,556 crore reported. It was ₹26,904 crore in FY2026, against ₹26,695 crore reported [CALCULATION: FIN-C033; FIN-C034]. FY2025 included a ₹14,323 crore Indus fair-value gain and an ₹8,573 crore deferred-tax-asset credit [FACT: FIN-C054]. Exceptional items have been non-zero every year since FY2014 and netted to −₹57,857 crore over FY2017–FY2026 [CALCULATION: FIN-C045].
5. **Owner earnings for FY2026 are estimated at ₹29,600–47,500 crore (base ~₹39,900 crore) for Airtel's shareholders** [ESTIMATE: FIN-C035]. This is above reported PAT, because D&A (₹52,711 crore) exceeds a depreciation-based maintenance-capex estimate plus normalised spectrum [FACT: FIN-C053]. It is below FY2026 equity FCF (₹53,054 crore), which was flattered by working-capital release and minimal spectrum outflow.
6. **Leverage has fallen sharply, but most of the remaining debt is owed to the government.** Net debt excluding leases was ₹94,249 crore, or 0.79× EBITDA, at Mar-2026. It was 4.26× at the FY2019 trough [CALCULATION: FIN-C026]. Including leases the ratio is 1.40×, and adding the non-indemnified AGR provision takes it to 1.53× [CALCULATION: FIN-C027]. DoT deferred payment liabilities (₹90,643 crore) make up almost all of net debt. Net financial debt excluding them was only ₹3,606 crore [CALCULATION: FIN-C028; FACT: FIN-C005].
7. **Liquidity is the one tight spot.** Liquid resources of ₹30,871 crore cover the ₹35,936 crore of gross borrowings due within 24 months only 0.86× [CALCULATION: FIN-C041; FACT: FIN-C008]. A 200 bp rate shock costs about ₹752 crore a year, because the DoT instalments carry fixed rates of 7.2–8 % [CALCULATION: FIN-C040]. The stress cases leave positive residual cash [CALCULATION: FIN-C039]. Refinancing ability therefore matters more than solvency.
8. **Per-share compounding over 10 years was moderate and diluted.** Revenue grew at 8.1 % a year but revenue per share at only 4.4 %. The rights-adjusted share count grew 3.5 % a year, through the 2019 rights issue and QIP and the 2021 rights issue. EPS CAGR was 12.5 % and BVPS CAGR 4.7 % [CALCULATION: FIN-C036; FIN-C037]. Equity issued (₹65,323 crore) and net NCI inflows (₹28,379 crore) funded the 2017–2021 trough [CALCULATION: FIN-C038].
9. **The India Mobile turnaround drives the economics. Africa remains a large, FX-exposed second engine.** The India Mobile segment result margin went from −13.7 % (FY2019) to 31.7 % (FY2026), with a 60.2 % segment EBITDA margin. Africa earned a 33.1 % result margin and supplied 26.9 % of group revenue [CALCULATION: FIN-C042]. Naira devaluation produced a ₹5,784 crore FX loss in FY2024 [FACT: FIN-C055].

## 4. Data Basis and Reconciliation

**Conclusion:** the series is a single consolidated, as-reported dataset with four documented breaks. Every ratio states which side of a break it sits on [INFERENCE].

- **Primary basis:** consolidated, in ₹ crore (filings report in ₹ million, divided by 10). Each year uses its audited annual-report figures. Where the following year's comparatives were restated, the differences are logged rather than silently replaced. Dataset: `specialists/financials_workpapers/financial_history.csv` (534 rows, 374 verified against tier-1). Builder: `calc/financials_build_history.py`. Metrics: `scripts/fin_metrics.py` gives `calc/financials_metrics.csv`. Supplementary calculations: `calc/financials_analysis.py` gives `calc/financials_analysis.out`.
- **Break 1, GAAP (FY2015 to FY2016):** FY2011–FY2015 are IFRS. For FY2016, IFRS and Ind AS revenue match exactly, but attributable profit differs by 10.8 % [FACT: FIN-C058; FIN-C017]. Profit comparisons across this break carry a ±10 % uncertainty band [INFERENCE: FIN-C058].
- **Break 2, IFRS 11 (FY2012 to FY2013):** FY2011–FY2012 consolidate 42 % of Indus proportionately. From FY2013 Indus is equity-accounted, which reduced FY2013 revenue by 4.2 % (see the verification log) [INFERENCE: FIN-C019].
- **Break 3, Ind AS 116 (FY2020):** lease rentals left opex, so the EBITDA margin rose from 31.9 % to 41.8 % [CALCULATION: FIN-C021]. Lease liabilities (₹73,740 crore at Mar-2026) are kept out of `total_debt` and shown separately [FACT: FIN-C005].
- **Break 4, Infratel/Indus scope (FY2021 and FY2025):** Infratel was deconsolidated in Nov-2020. FY2021 P&L lines are continuing operations, and PAT includes a ₹11,057 crore discontinued profit [FACT: FIN-C015]. Indus became a subsidiary from 19-Nov-2024, adding ₹21,767 crore of NCI [FACT: FIN-C012]. Q3FY2025–Q2FY2026 year-on-year growth is therefore inflated by consolidation.
- **Telecom definitions used:** `ebitda` = revenue − operating expenses, excluding other income. The company's "profit before D&A" includes other income [CALCULATION: FIN-C002]. `capex` excludes spectrum, and spectrum cash, lease principal and interest paid (a financing-section item for Airtel) are tracked separately [FACT: FIN-C004]. `total_debt` includes DoT deferred payment liabilities for spectrum and AGR [FACT: FIN-C005; FIN-C007]. `cash_and_equivalents` excludes other bank balances, most of which are restricted mobile-money trust balances [CALCULATION: FIN-C041]. Per-share data are adjusted for the bonus element of the 2019 and 2021 rights issues using the company's own restatement factors [CALCULATION: FIN-C036].
- **Standalone vs. consolidated:** not mixed in any ratio. Industrial and financing economics need no separation, because no consolidated financing arm is material [INFERENCE].

## 6. Revenue and Margin Drivers

**Conclusion:** revenue growth since FY2021 (16.0 % CAGR over 5 years, against 8.1 % over 10 years) comes from India Mobile pricing and mix recovery after the 2016–2019 price war, Africa growth, and from FY2025 the Indus consolidation. The margin expansion is real operating leverage on a largely fixed cost base, not just the lease-accounting effect [INFERENCE: FIN-C021; FIN-C042; FIN-C036].

- **Price-war trough:** group revenue fell 16.3 % and EBITDA 24.2 % between FY2016 and FY2019 [CALCULATION: FIN-C044]. India Mobile revenue fell 26.5 % between FY2017 and FY2019 [CALCULATION: FIN-C043].
- **Recovery:** India Mobile revenue grew at 15.4 % a year from FY2019 to FY2026, and Africa (in ₹) at 14.9 % (calc section J) [CALCULATION: FIN-C042]. The EBITDA margin rose from 45.1 % (FY2021, post Ind AS 116) to 56.7 % (FY2026). Ind AS 116 cannot explain this, because it was already in both years [CALCULATION: FIN-C021].
- **Cost structure:** access charges and licence/spectrum fees, the main revenue-linked costs, were ₹20,915 crore in FY2026, or 9.9 % of revenue [CALCULATION: FIN-C066]. Network opex (₹39,750 crore) and other costs are largely fixed. Incremental revenue therefore falls through at high margins, and the same leverage works in reverse in a volume shock (§11) [INFERENCE: FIN-C039].
- **Other income** was 1.3 % of revenue in FY2026 and 6.2 % of PBT, which is not a material dependency [CALCULATION: FIN-C066].

## 8. Cash Conversion and Working Capital

**Conclusion:** operating cash conversion is very high, because D&A is large and working capital is structurally negative. The heavy claims of spectrum, leases and interest, however, mean that equity-level cash has historically been far below PAT. ## 16. Four-Layer Evidence Crosswalk

### Layer 1 — Bedrock Compounder
- **Relevant evidence:** through-cycle ROIC, incremental ROIC, per-share compounding over 10–15 years, reinvestment capacity.
- **Supports:** incremental ROIC of 23–28 % on capital added since FY2021 [CALCULATION: FIN-C025]; ROIC at a record 13.9 % and ROCE at 18.8 % [CALCULATION: FIN-C024; FIN-C022]; equity FCF finally well above PAT in FY2026 [CALCULATION: FIN-C032].

- **Domain implications:** the numbers support a company in its first strong phase of compounding (FY2022–FY2026), not a proven multi-cycle compounder. Five years of high incremental returns sit against ten years of capital destruction or stagnation.
- **Missing information:** a cost-of-capital hurdle (valuation); evidence that returns hold through the next tariff or technology cycle; maintenance capex for 5G densification.
- **Plausibility effect:** No change. Current and incremental returns raise it, but through-cycle returns and per-share dilution over ten years lower it by a similar amount.

### Layer 2 — High-Growth Business
- **Relevant evidence:** revenue and margin scaling, operating leverage, capital intensity of growth.
- **Supports:** 16.0 % revenue CAGR over 5 years with the EBITDA margin rising from 45.1 % to 56.7% [CALCULATION: FIN-C021; FIN-C036]; India Mobile capex intensity falling to 16.2 % of revenue [CALCULATION: FIN-C047]; Q1FY2027 organic growth of 18.4 % [FACT: FIN-C051].

- **Domain implications:** this looks like a mature operator growing through pricing, with strong operating leverage. It does not look like an adoption-driven, high-growth business.
- **Missing information:** split of growth into ARPU and subscribers, and constant-currency Africa growth (concalls/industry).
- **Plausibility effect:** Lowers. Growth is well above nominal GDP for now, but it comes from price repair and consolidation rather than a long adoption runway visible in the financials.

### Layer 3 — Cyclical / Event-Driven Opportunity
- **Relevant evidence:** peak, trough and mid-cycle margins and returns; trough solvency; normalised vs. current earnings.
- **Supports:** a full cycle is visible. Margin trough at 31.9 % (FY2019) [CALCULATION: FIN-C021], ROCE trough at 2.5 % [CALCULATION: FIN-C022], recovery to record levels [CALCULATION: FIN-C044]. On a mid-cycle basis (EBITDA margin 30.2–37.0 % pre-lease in FY2011–FY2018 [CALCULATION: FIN-C065]; ROIC mostly 5–9 %), current earnings are well above normal [CALCULATION: FIN-C024; FIN-C021]. Trough survival needed equity issuance [FACT: FIN-C016].
- **Domain implications:** if the industry structure stays rational, current economics may be a new plateau rather than a cyclical peak. The financials alone cannot tell which. The cyclical risk runs to the downside (a mean-reversion of tariffs).
- **Missing information:** industry structure and tariff outlook (industry, moat); the valuation-implied margin.
- **Plausibility effect:** Raises. The record shows a deep, full cycle, and today's numbers sit at the top of it, which makes the cyclical and normalisation question decision-relevant.

- **Relevant evidence:** runway, burn, dilution, the share of value from unproven segments.
- **Supports:** small segments with optional upside exist inside the group. Homes is in a build-out phase (₹6,810 crore capex in FY2026 on ₹7,775 crore revenue) [FACT: FIN-C052]. ## 20. Domain Conclusion

1. **Through-cycle and incremental returns:** through-cycle ROIC (25.17 % tax) was about 2–9 % between FY2012 and FY2021 and is 13.9 % now. Incremental ROIC on capital added over the last 3–5 years is 23–28 % [CALCULATION: FIN-C024; FIN-C025].
2. **Cash conversion:** CFO/PAT is 4.2× over 5 years and 8.6× over 10. After spectrum, leases and interest, equity FCF is 0.72× and 0.23× of PAT. The gap is the spectrum, network and lease burden of a capital-intensive operator, and FY2026 is the first year of a strong surplus [CALCULATION: FIN-C030; FIN-C031; FIN-C032].
3. **Owner earnings:** ₹29,600–47,500 crore (base ~₹39,900 crore) for FY2026, against reported attributable PAT of ₹26,695 crore and underlying ₹26,904 crore [ESTIMATE: FIN-C035; CALCULATION: FIN-C033].
4. **Worst years and resilience:** in FY2019–FY2020 revenue fell 16 %, EBITDA 24 %, net debt/EBITDA reached 4.3× and interest cover 0.4×. Survival needed ₹39,181 crore of new equity. Today net debt is 0.8× EBITDA (1.4× including leases), mostly fixed-rate DoT instalments. Liquidity covers 24-month maturities 0.86×, a gap FCF covers [CALCULATION: FIN-C044; FIN-C026; FIN-C041; FACT: FIN-C016].
5. **Capital allocation per share:** over 10 years, a 3.5 % a year share-count increase and 4.7 % a year BVPS growth. Value per share was not compounded strongly over the decade, but the capital deployed since FY2021 is earning well [CALCULATION: FIN-C036; FIN-C025].
6. **Crosswalk inputs:** strong operating leverage and falling capital intensity; current earnings at a 16-year peak relative to mid-cycle history; no funding need [INFERENCE: FIN-C021; FIN-C047].
