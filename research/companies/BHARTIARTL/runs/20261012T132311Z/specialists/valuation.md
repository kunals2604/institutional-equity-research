## Key Findings

1. **The price needs bull-case operations or a much lower discount rate.**
   - At the run's 11.0% WACC, the base consolidated DCF gives about INR 1,215 per share, within a range of INR 1,145–1,342 [CALCULATION: VAL-C044]. That puts the price 43% above the base midpoint (margin of safety −43%) [CALCULATION: VAL-C049].
   - The price roughly equals the bull DCF (INR 1,887; range 1,690–2,123) [CALCULATION: VAL-C046].
   - Holding base operations, the price implies a WACC of 9.5% (cost of equity about 9.9%, only 2.5 pp above a 7.39% G-Sec) [CALCULATION: VAL-C052; FACT: VAL-C023].
2. **Reverse DCF: market expectations are Demanding.**
   - The price requires 14.4% consolidated revenue growth every year for 10 years [CALCULATION: VAL-C042]. That compares with 8.1% over the last 10 years and 8.8% over 15 years [CALCULATION: VAL-C043].
   - Alternatively, it requires the EBIT margin to rise from 32.6% to 51.9% [CALCULATION: VAL-C042].
   - The only comparable stretch of history is the 5-year tariff-repair period (16.0% CAGR, FY2021–26), which started from a depressed base [CALCULATION: VAL-C043].
3. **Expected return is below the hurdle.**
   - E[IRR] over 5 years is 4.7%, against an 11.7% hurdle (cost of equity) [CALCULATION: VAL-C049; CALCULATION: VAL-C026].
   - The return decomposition gives a higher figure of about 8% probability-weighted (base 9.0%), because it assumes 15% EPS growth and a P/E of 28x in five years [ESTIMATE: VAL-C048]. It is still below the hurdle.
   - E[IRR] reaches the hurdle at a price of about INR 1,300, 27% below today [CALCULATION: VAL-C051].
4. **The multiples are full, not extreme.**
   - Trailing EV/EBITDA is 10.0x on the company's own basis and 10.9x with minorities at market [CALCULATION: VAL-C031].
   - The 17-year range (FY2010–26) is 6.65–11.83x, with a median of 8.99x. The current multiple sits at the 71st percentile [CALCULATION: VAL-C030; CALCULATION: VAL-C031].
   - Trailing P/E of 38.3x is near the median of the meaningful years [CALCULATION: VAL-C031].
   - Margins are at a 17-year high: the EBIT margin is 32.6% vs. 5.9–31.4% before. A full multiple on record margins is the main de-rating risk [CALCULATION: VAL-C043].
5. **The market prices India at a premium and Africa at a discount.**
   - Airtel Africa's market value implies about 6.0x TTM EBITDA [CALCULATION: VAL-C053; CALCULATION: VAL-C034].
   - The India business, including Indus and Hexacom, is therefore priced at about 12.4x TTM EBITDA [CALCULATION: VAL-C053].
   - The reported Jio IPO target (about INR 11 lakh crore, about 36.6x FY2026 PAT) frames the domestic peer anchor. The IPO is also a near-term supply event [FACT: VAL-C038; FACT: VAL-C056].
6. **Per-share history is weaker than aggregate history.**
   - The share count rose 56% between FY2016 and June 2026 (rights issues, QIP, preferential issues; most recently 146.8 mn shares to the promoter-group entity ICIL for 16.31% of Airtel Africa at INR 1,923 per share) [CALCULATION: VAL-C043; FACT: VAL-C004].
   - As a result, revenue per share compounded at only 3.7% a year over ten years [CALCULATION: VAL-C043].
   - The dilutive overhang is now negligible: diluted EPS of 13.37 vs. basic EPS of 13.38 in Q1FY27 [FACT: VAL-C012].
7. **Strong 10-year shareholder returns, with deep drawdowns in the cycle.**
   - TSR was 20.9% a year over 10 years and 21.8% over 5 years, against 11.2% and 7.1% price CAGR for the Nifty 500 [CALCULATION: VAL-C018].
   - Over 15 and 20 years, TSR was 12.4% and 11.7%, roughly in line with the index [CALCULATION: VAL-C018].
   - The stock fell 56.9% in 2007–2012 and took about 12 years to recover. A further 46.8% drawdown came during the Jio price war (2017–18) [CALCULATION: VAL-C019].
   - The price is 17.8% below the November 2025 high [CALCULATION: VAL-C021].
8. **Liquidity is not a constraint.** 90-day ADTV is about INR 1,209 crore, so the INR 50 lakh reference position exits in a fraction of one day at 20% participation [CALCULATION: VAL-C013; CALCULATION: VAL-C014]. Gate G5 is not triggered.

## Method Selection

**Lifecycle (T15) [JUDGMENT]:** Airtel is a mature-to-late-growth incumbent in a consolidated three-private-player Indian market, plus a high-growth, FX-exposed Africa business.
- Consolidated revenue grew 16% a year over five years (tariff repair, Africa, Indus consolidation) but 8–9% over 10–15 years [CALCULATION: VAL-C043].
- Capital intensity remains high: capex is 22–30% of revenue in FY2018–26 [CALCULATION: VAL-C043].

| Method | Used for | Why it fits | Why not alone |
|---|---|---|---|
| Consolidated FCFF DCF with fade, terminal value via RONIC (primary) | L1 base, bear and bull | Cash generation is now large and predictable. The capex cycle is captured through the sales-to-capital ratio. Leases are treated as debt, consistent with EBIT | The terminal value is 49–62% of EV, and the result is very sensitive to the discount rate |
| Reverse DCF (growth, margin, implied WACC) | T12 expectations | Shows what the price needs, independent of my base case | Single-variable solves overstate any one driver |
| Lease-consistent EV/EBITDA, own 17-year history, and segment-implied (India vs Africa at market) | Cross-check, L3 | Sector playbook §16; regime-aware | Margins are at record highs, so the multiple flatters |
| SOTP with listed subsidiaries at market (look-through) | L3, holdco lens | Airtel Africa, Indus and Hexacom are listed; the minority claims are large (about INR 1.09 lakh crore) | Uses market prices of the subsidiaries, which are themselves uncertain |
| Expected-return decomposition | Sanity check | Ties the DCF to EPS and the multiple | Rests on assumed EPS growth and exit P/E |
| Outcome tree for adjacencies | L4 note | Data centres (Nxtra), Airtel Money/fintech, NBFC | Too small and undisclosed to value separately; qualitative only |

**Methods rejected:** P/B (book equity is distorted by the AGR losses and the Indus purchase accounting); a dividend discount model (payout is low while capex is heavy); peak-margin multiple valuation without normalisation.

**Setup.** This is a long-duration consolidated FCFF DCF with:

- terminal growth of 5%, below the 7.39% risk-free rate [ESTIMATE: VAL-C041].

**Base result:** INR 1,215 per share, range INR 1,145–1,342 [CALCULATION: VAL-C044].
- The terminal value is 56% of EV, and the implied exit EV/EBIT is 8.2x [CALCULATION: VAL-C044].

**Competitive-advantage period (CAP) implied by the price [INFERENCE: VAL-C042; VAL-C052]:** at today's price, the market needs either:
- returns well above the cost of capital for longer than 10 years, with growth close to the bull case; or
- a cost of equity of about 9.9%.

**CAP the evidence supports [INFERENCE: VAL-C019; VAL-C043]:** excess returns have existed only since FY2022. The last episode of irrational competition (2016–19) was eight years ago. A durable but contestable advantage supports the base case, not a 15–20-year CAP.

### Layer 2 — High-Growth Business

**Lens:** a scale-stage scenario DCF. The bull DCF stands in for the L2 path:
- revenue growth of 14% fading to 8%, roughly 3.3x revenue in 10 years;
- EBIT margin rising to 42%;
- incremental sales-to-capital of 2.0.

**Result:** INR 1,887 per share (range 1,690–2,123) [CALCULATION: VAL-C046].

**Reachable market [INFERENCE: VAL-C054; VAL-C040]:** India mobile ARPU growth runs at about 5% a year without tariff rounds, while the homes, enterprise and data-centre businesses are growing quickly from small bases. Sustaining 14% for a decade would need repeated tariff rounds plus Africa compounding in INR. Note that Africa grew 45% year on year in INR in Q1FY27, helped by currency effects.

**Crosswalk finding [JUDGMENT]:** the price needs L2-type growth (VAL-C042: 14.4% for 10 years) from a business whose 10- and 15-year economics look like L1/L3 (8–9% revenue growth, 3.7% revenue-per-share growth).

### Layer 3 — Cyclical / Event-Driven Opportunity

**Lens:** normalised value, SOTP and a downside floor.

**Normalised earnings.** Over FY2010–FY2026 the EBIT margin averaged far below today's 32.6% (range 5.9–31.4%) [CALCULATION: VAL-C043]. If tariff discipline is now structural, the current margin is the new normal, and that is the base case. If it is not, a 20–25% EBIT margin (the FY2022–23 level) at about 8x EV/EBITDA leads to the bear value of INR 627–787 [CALCULATION: VAL-C045].

**SOTP / holdco view [CALCULATION: VAL-C032; CALCULATION: VAL-C034; CALCULATION: VAL-C053]:**
- Airtel's look-through stakes at market: Airtel Africa about INR 1.06 lakh crore, Indus about INR 51,000 crore, Hexacom about INR 53,000 crore. Together, about 19% of Airtel's market cap.
- Africa at 6x EV/EBITDA is not an obvious source of hidden value at the group level, because the market already pays for it.
- Any SOTP upside depends on India being worth more than 12.4x EBITDA.
- Unannounced events, such as an Airtel Money listing or further Indus stake purchases, are not valued (special-situations owns them).

**Downside floor [CALCULATION: VAL-C045; CALCULATION: VAL-C050]:** the bear DCF of INR 627–787 is the floor. The 5-year bear outcome including dividends is −33% to −41% from today.

### Layer 4 — Venture-Style / Optionality Bet
