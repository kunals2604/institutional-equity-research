## 2. Key Findings

1. **No hard catalyst drives Airtel's own per-share value in the next 12 months. The event most likely to change the thesis is a soft one, Indian mobile tariff repair, and it has no date.** There has been no headline tariff round since July 2024. ARPU has still risen 7.8% in five quarters on mix and premiumisation (INR 245 to INR 264) [CALCULATION: SPS-C069]. Management itself says the pricing "architecture" needs repair and that Airtel "cannot do it single-handedly" [MANAGEMENT STATEMENT: SPS-C060]. The market rewarded the 2021 tariff round with a +7.7% excess move; the 2024 round drew -1.9%, which suggests the market already expected it [CALCULATION: SPS-C080]. [JUDGMENT]
2. **The holding-company/SOTP angle is small relative to Airtel's size.** All listed stakes (Airtel Africa 79.11%, Bharti Hexacom 70%, Indus 51.256%) are worth about INR 209,615 crore, or INR 336 per share: 18.9% of market cap [CALCULATION: SPS-C075]. A 20-30% holding discount on the Africa stake would be worth only about INR 34-51 per share (2-3%) if it closed. The market is valuing the India operating business, at about 12.6x annualised EBITDAaL [CALCULATION: SPS-C078]. Discount closure is not a thesis here. [JUDGMENT]
3. **Airtel Money's London IPO (priced at GBP 1.96, about USD 7.0bn; admission expected 14-Oct-2026) is a Hard, dated event, but its value-realization mechanism for Airtel shareholders is weak.** The offer is entirely secondary: minority investors sell, Airtel Africa sells nothing outside the over-allotment, and no cash comes to the Group [FACT: SPS-C043; SPS-C044; SPS-C045]. Its main effects are a public mark (Airtel's look-through stake is worth about INR 66 per share [CALCULATION: SPS-C076]) and the effective removal of the minority put-option overhang [FACT: SPS-C019; SPS-C039]. Airtel Africa fell from 309.6p to 290.2p on the first day of conditional dealings [FACT: SPS-C047]. [INFERENCE: SPS-C047; SPS-C065]
4. **The cycle turnaround is complete and visible in audited and company-reported numbers. Airtel is now in a late-recovery or expansion phase, not at an inflection.** EBITDA fell 26.2% from FY2017 to the FY2019 trough, then compounded about 24% a year to FY2026 (mixed accounting basis). India mobile ARPU fell 47.5% from the pre-Jio high to INR 104 and is now INR 264, 2.54x the trough [CALCULATION: SPS-C069]. Net debt to EBITDA went from 4.40x (FY2019, LTM) to 1.36x (FY2026) [FACT: SPS-C083; SPS-C055], and S&P moved to BBB+ [FACT: SPS-C057]. [JUDGMENT]
5. **The largest recent corporate action is a promoter-related share swap: ICIL received 146.76m new Airtel shares (INR 28,222 crore) for 16.31% of Airtel Africa.** It raised Airtel's stake to about 79% and cost no cash [FACT: SPS-C035; SPS-C036]. At signing, the terms favoured Airtel's minority shareholders: Airtel paid about 365p per Airtel Africa share, 11.7% below market, using its own shares issued 9.5% above market. Marked to today's prices, however, Airtel gave about INR 26,104 crore of stock for Airtel Africa shares now worth INR 21,759 crore, because Airtel Africa has fallen 30% since then [CALCULATION: SPS-C079]. [JUDGMENT]
6. **Capital is still being raised and recycled at the edges, with recurring minority and promoter co-investment.** Nxtra raised USD 1bn at about USD 3.1bn post-money; Airtel's estimated stake falls to about 61% [FACT: SPS-C041] [ESTIMATE: SPS-C077]. The new NBFC, Airtel Money Limited, is 30% owned by the promoter vehicle Bharti Enterprises [FACT: SPS-C040]. The partly paid rights final call raised INR 15,696 crore in March 2026 [FACT: SPS-C031]. Per-share value creation therefore has to be judged after these leakages. [INFERENCE: SPS-C040; SPS-C041]
7. **Management's record on executing events is mixed but improving.** Telenor, TTSL and the Indus control step were executed. Value-realization claims have sometimes not materialised: the April 2021 demerger "to unlock value" was withdrawn in January 2022 [FACT: SPS-C014], and the 2025 Tata Play DTH merger talks were terminated [FACT: SPS-C061]. The 2019-2021 equity raises (rights issues at INR 220 and INR 535, a QIP at INR 445) funded survival and AGR [FACT: SPS-C008; SPS-C009; SPS-C015; SPS-C012]. [JUDGMENT]
8. **The downside floor is far below the price.** At Airtel's own trough multiples of 6.65-8.0x, applied to current India EBITDAaL, equity value is INR 915-1,113 per share (-49% to -37%), and INR 685-853 under a 15% earnings shock with a 50% haircut on Africa [CALCULATION: SPS-C082]. The protection is earnings durability, not assets. [JUDGMENT]

## 4. Corporate-Action History

**Conclusion.** Over FY2016-FY2027, Airtel's corporate actions fall into four phases. (a) FY2016-FY2019: in-market consolidation (Telenor, TTSL, spectrum trading) and asset sales (Infratel stakes, Africa exits) to absorb the Jio shock. (b) FY2020-FY2022: recapitalisation at depressed prices (rights issues, QIP, FCCB) to fund AGR dues and spectrum. (c) FY2022-FY2025: deleveraging (spectrum prepayments, perpetual and bond redemptions) and taking control of Indus. (d) FY2025-FY2027: consolidating minorities in Airtel Africa and raising outside or promoter money for new ventures (Nxtra, the NBFC). Execution was good on mergers and integration. Promises to "unlock value" were not reliably kept. [JUDGMENT: SPS-C014; SPS-C061]

**What the history says about management's event execution.** Airtel reliably closes mergers and integrates them: Telenor, TTSL and Indus all became operating fact. It funds strategic needs with equity when it has to, at prices that in hindsight were low (INR 220, INR 445, INR 535). The FY2012 to FY2019 market cap stayed roughly flat at INR 1,279-1,403bn in a period that included the 2019 rights issue [FACT: SPS-C083], so dilution then destroyed per-share value. The recurring pattern since FY2025 is *buying back minorities* (DTH, Airtel Africa, Indus) and *selling minorities in new ventures* (Nxtra, Airtel Money, the NBFC). That is capital recycling, not a catalyst. Announcements framed as "value unlocking" (the 2021 demerger, the DTH merger) have a poor record of completion. [INFERENCE: SPS-C014; SPS-C061; SPS-C007; SPS-C024; SPS-C041]

**Leakage and cash access (why a discount could be justified, and why it is small).**
- *Two layers of minorities* sit on Airtel Money (79.11% x 77.85%), and Airtel Africa has its own operating-company minorities (e.g. INR 32,135m of NCI inside the Airtel Africa structure at Mar-2026) [FACT: SPS-C029; SPS-C043; SPS-C037]. - *Cash access* is real but partial. Airtel's policy passes 100% of net dividend income from subsidiaries to its shareholders [FACT: SPS-C034]. **Discount-closure mechanism test (T10).** No board approval, scheme, offer document or management statement points to a spin-off, distribution in specie, sale of the Africa stake, or a take-private of Airtel Africa. The Board's stated aim for the swap is "alignment" and a "simplified" structure [MANAGEMENT STATEMENT: SPS-C036]. **Verdict: no mechanism; immaterial in any case.** [JUDGMENT: SPS-C036]

**ICIL swap: a related-party test.** Airtel issued 146.76m shares (2.35%) to a promoter entity [FACT: SPS-C035] [CALCULATION: SPS-C089]. At announcement, the price favoured Airtel's minority holders: an 11.7% discount to Airtel Africa's market price, paid in Airtel stock issued at 9.5% above its market price. Since then, Airtel Africa's 30% fall has made the trade value-negative mark-to-market (INR 26,104 crore given vs INR 21,759 crore received) [CALCULATION: SPS-C079]. Two further points: (a) the promoter group swapped a direct Africa holding into Airtel paper, which increases its concentration in the parent; (b) Airtel's minority holders now carry more Africa FX and political risk. I see no evidence of value transfer *at signing*. Governance specialists should review the valuation report and the independent-director process. [INFERENCE: SPS-C079; SPS-C036]
