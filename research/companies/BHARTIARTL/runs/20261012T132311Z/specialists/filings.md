# Bharti Airtel Limited (BHARTIARTL): Filings and Forensic-Accounting Report

All amounts are **INR million as filed** unless stated (INR 1 crore = INR 10 million). The basis is **consolidated** unless marked standalone. FY2026 is the year ending 31-Mar-2026. Indus Towers has been a subsidiary since the close of 18-Nov-2024, so consolidated series break in FY2025.

## Mandate, Scope and Independence

**Decision questions answered.**
1. Are accounting policies and estimates conservative or aggressive, and by how much?
2. Do related-party flows transfer value away from minority holders?
3. What contingent, off-balance-sheet, legal, regulatory and ESG exposures exist, and what could they cost?
4. What do the auditor reports, KAMs, CARO/IFC reports and auditor history show?
5. Does consolidation distort the economics?
6. What does the rating history say?
7. Overall, how reliable is the reported information?

## Key Findings

1. **Reported profit has been heavily shaped by items the company labels "exceptional".**
   - Over FY2017-FY2026, the cumulative net exceptional charge was INR 578,571 mn, which equals 0.88x cumulative consolidated PAT. Exceptional items were a net charge in 7 of 10 years [CALCULATION: FIL-C033; FIL-C032].
   - Most of these charges are recurring regulatory levies, AGR and FX devaluation. Examples are INR 31,607 mn in FY2026 and INR 63,586 mn in FY2025 [FACT: FIL-C007; FIL-C008].
   - "Pre-exceptional" profit therefore overstates through-cycle owner earnings for this company [JUDGMENT].
2. **The last two years of headline profit contain large non-operating credits.**
   - FY2025 attributable PAT of INR 335,561 mn included an INR 107,025 mn fair-value gain on the Indus stake and an INR 85,732 mn deferred-tax-asset recognition. These equal 31.9% and 25.5% of FY2025 attributable PAT respectively [CALCULATION: FIL-C038; FIL-C008; FIL-C009].
   - In FY2026, an INR 30,952 mn deferred-tax-liability reversal (11.6% of attributable PAT) was booked in the same quarter as an INR 31,607 mn regulatory-levy charge, so the two largely offset [FACT: FIL-C058].
3. **The audit record is clean on opinion, but judgment areas recur.**
   - Deloitte Haskins & Sells LLP gave unmodified opinions in every year from FY2018 to FY2026, and S.R. Batliboi did so in FY2017 [FACT: FIL-C001; FIL-C040].
   - Every consolidated report from FY2017 to FY2024 nonetheless carried an Emphasis of Matter: the one-time spectrum charge in FY2017-FY2019, then the going-concern uncertainty at Indus's largest customer, Vodafone Idea, in FY2020-FY2024 [FACT: FIL-C042].
   - Regulatory and tax contingencies have been a KAM in every year since FY2020 [FACT: FIL-C043; FIL-C024].
   - Deloitte retires at the 2027 AGM, and the board has approved S.R. Batliboi to return [FACT: FIL-C041].
4. **Government-dues exposure is large and only partly visible in the contingent-liability total.**
   - The contingent-liability table shows INR 191,842 mn (12.9% of owners' equity) [FACT: FIL-C010; CALCULATION: FIL-C036].
   - Disclosed separately, outside that table: an OTSC balance of INR 66,065 mn and an ISP licence-fee demand of INR 48,619 mn [FACT: FIL-C011].
   - Already provided on the balance sheet: an AGR sub-judice provision of INR 347,042 mn, 55.2% of which is indemnified by Tata and Telenor [FACT: FIL-C012; CALCULATION: FIL-C039]; disputed trade payables of INR 245,350 mn [FACT: FIL-C014]; and deferred spectrum and AGR liabilities of INR 906,430 mn inside borrowings [FACT: FIL-C013].
   - On OTSC alone, interest accrued (INR 121,377 mn) is 6.7x the principal charged [CALCULATION: FIL-C037].
5. **Cash-flow classification flatters reported CFO.**
   - Lease payments (INR 67,197 mn), interest (INR 148,876 mn) and AGR principal (INR 54,272 mn) all sit in financing. Together they equal 22.1% of FY2026 CFO.
   - After these, FY2026 free cash flow falls from INR 746,608 mn to INR 476,263 mn [CALCULATION: FIL-C035; FACT: FIL-C015; FIL-C016].
6. **A large promoter-group transaction followed the year-end.**
   - In Q1FY2027 Airtel issued 146,761,335 shares (INR 282,222 mn) to Indian Continent Investment Ltd, a promoter-group entity, for 16.31% of Airtel Africa. Group ownership of Airtel Africa rose to 78.93% [FACT: FIL-C063; MANAGEMENT STATEMENT: FIL-C065].
   - The reported terms favour minorities on market prices: a 9.5% premium on the Airtel shares issued and an 11.6% discount on the Airtel Africa shares acquired [FACT: FIL-C066]. The valuer's report and the EGM notice were not retrieved.
   - The FY2026 Ind AS 24 note lists ICIL only as a "voluntary" other related party [FACT: FIL-C022].
7. **The credit trajectory is clearly improving.**
   - Airtel was cut to AA (CRISIL) and Ba1 (Moody's, below investment grade) after the 2016-2019 price war [FACT: FIL-C046].
   - It has since reached CRISIL AAA (Jul-2025), Moody's Baa2 and S&P BBB in FY2026 [FACT: FIL-C044], and BBB+ at S&P in June 2026 per a news report [FACT: FIL-C049].
   - CRISIL's downgrade trigger is net leverage, including deferred dues and leases, sustained above 2.5-2.7x; leverage was 2.1x in FY2025 [FACT: FIL-C047].
8. **The promoter holding structure creates a dividend dependence.**
   - Bharti Telecom and its parents carry about INR 39,975 crore of gross debt, serviced from Airtel dividends. The Airtel shares are unencumbered [FACT: FIL-C048].
   - Airtel paid INR 37,737 mn of dividends to Bharti Telecom in FY2026 [FACT: FIL-C022].

## Accounting Policies and Estimates

**Conclusion.** The core accounting choices sit within normal telecom practice, and several estimate changes went in the conservative direction. The material reliability issues are elsewhere [JUDGMENT: FIL-C053; FIL-C033]:
- what is labelled "exceptional";
- large judgment-driven one-off credits (purchase-price allocation and deferred tax);
- regulatory-provision estimates.

**Useful lives.** Re-estimates moved in both directions:

| Year | Change | Direction for profit | Amount |
|---|---|---|---|
| FY2017 | Network-asset lives shortened | Lower profit | INR 9,196 mn, of which 2,920 booked as exceptional |
| FY2019 (standalone) | Certain network assets lengthened from 18 to 25 years | Higher profit | Depreciation INR 2,538 mn lower |
| FY2020 | Network assets shortened | Lower profit | +INR 11,524 mn |
| FY2020 | DTH CPE lengthened from 5 to 7 years | Higher profit | -INR 2,188 mn |
| FY2021 | Network assets shortened again | Lower profit | +INR 8,345 mn |
| FY2022-FY2026 | No change located | — | — |

Net, the disclosed re-estimates increased depreciation [FACT: FIL-C053]. That is conservative-leaning [INFERENCE: FIL-C053].

**Borrowing-cost capitalisation (spectrum).**
- Interest on deferred spectrum is capitalised until the spectrum is put to use. The amounts were INR 2,840 mn in FY2022, INR 14,752 mn in FY2023 and INR 13,654 mn in FY2024, then INR 3,958 mn in FY2025 and nil in FY2026 [FACT: FIL-C050; FIL-C051; FIL-C052].
- The FY2023-FY2024 amounts relate to the 5G auction. They raised reported PBT in those years, and the interest now flows through amortisation instead [INFERENCE: FIL-C050].
- The policy is Ind AS 23-compliant. The point is comparability: FY2023-FY2024 finance costs are understated relative to FY2026 [JUDGMENT].

**Business-combination accounting (Indus, FY2025).**
- Remeasuring the existing 50% stake to fair value produced a gain of INR 107,025 mn. The purchase-price allocation recognised goodwill of INR 208,593 mn, measured NCI at its proportionate share (INR 217,667 mn), and treated trade receivables of INR 95,071 mn as fully collectible [FACT: FIL-C017].
- Under the proportionate-NCI method, goodwill is lower than under the full-goodwill method.
- The gain is non-cash and arose from a change in board composition, not from a purchase [INFERENCE: FIL-C017].
- Deloitte made the purchase-price allocation a KAM in FY2025, noting level III inputs [FACT: FIL-C030].

**Deferred tax.**
- FY2025: recognition of previously unrecognised deferred-tax assets on tax losses "due to favourable orders" (INR 85,732 mn consolidated; INR 84,850 mn standalone).
- FY2026: reversal of deferred-tax liabilities on investments (INR 30,952 mn) [FACT: FIL-C009; FIL-C026].
- Standalone deferred-tax assets fell from INR 190,412 mn to INR 132,359 mn as losses were used, with nil current tax in FY2026 [FACT: FIL-C027; FIL-C026].
- DTA recoverability was a KAM in FY2020-FY2023 [FACT: FIL-C043].
- These are estimate-heavy credits that are not repeatable [JUDGMENT].

**Hyperinflation and FX.**
- Malawi has been accounted for as hyperinflationary under Ind AS 29 since 1-Apr-2024. Non-monetary assets were uplifted by INR 65,028 mn, and the FY2026 after-tax effect was a loss of INR 972 mn [FACT: FIL-C019].
- Naira devaluation losses were classified as exceptional: INR 5,988 mn net in FY2025 for Nigeria and Tanzania [FACT: FIL-C008]. The naira was also a FY2024 KAM [FACT: FIL-C043].
- Recurring FX losses classified as exceptional are an "exceptional-in-name" pattern [INFERENCE: FIL-C032].

## Revenue and Expense Recognition

**Conclusion.** The tests show no sign of premature or aggressive recognition [INFERENCE: FIL-C069]. Revenue is prepaid-heavy and largely cash-backed:
- Closing DSO, including non-current receivables, was 14.2 days in FY2026 against 16.2 in FY2025.
- Unbilled revenue was stable at 2.13% of revenue.
- Deferred revenue (customer prepayments) was 7.1% of revenue [CALCULATION: FIL-C069; FACT: FIL-C068].
- Receivables grew 7.3% against revenue growth of 22.0%. Revenue growth is inflated by the full-year consolidation of Indus [CALCULATION: FIL-C069].
- The bad-debt charge was INR 3,586 mn, 0.17% of revenue [FACT: FIL-C068].

**Where revenue judgment does exist:**
- **Indus tower revenue.** Indus has "multiple reconciliation matters" with customers and estimates potential revenue reversals, which is a KAM [FACT: FIL-C001]. Vodafone Idea is a significant share of Indus revenue and receivables [FACT: FIL-C018].
- **IT-system complexity of prepaid revenue.** This has been a KAM every year [FACT: FIL-C043].
- **Expense side.** Regulatory levies (licence fee and SUC) are periodically "re-assessed". Under-accruals are then charged as exceptional, not as operating cost [FACT: FIL-C058; FIL-C007]. Recurring levy true-ups booked below EBITDA flatter reported EBITDA margins [INFERENCE: FIL-C032].

**ICIL share swap: test and alternative explanations.**
- **Mechanics.** 0.2466 Airtel shares were issued per Airtel Africa share, implying INR 474.2 per Airtel Africa share. The consideration was 5.7x the NCI carrying value derecognised. The new shares equal 2.35% of the fully paid share count [CALCULATION: FIL-C067].
- **Benchmark.** On reported market prices, the Airtel shares were issued at a 9.5% premium and the Airtel Africa shares acquired at an 11.6% discount, with a registered valuer's swap ratio and price above the ICDR floor [FACT: FIL-C066]. Shareholders approved it with near-unanimous support [MANAGEMENT STATEMENT: FIL-C065].
- **Reading on that evidence.** The transaction looks favourable to Airtel minorities, not a transfer to the promoter [INFERENCE: FIL-C066; FIL-C067].
- **What could make it less favourable.** Consolidating Airtel Africa raises Airtel's exposure to African FX and country risk. ICIL converts a less liquid LSE stake into Airtel stock. Market prices before the relevant date may not reflect intrinsic value on either side.
- **Evidence that would resolve it.** The valuer's report, the EGM explanatory statement, and promoter-group abstention on the vote. None were retrieved [JUDGMENT].
- **Disclosure point.** The company calls ICIL a promoter-group entity [MANAGEMENT STATEMENT: FIL-C065], but the FY2026 RPT note places it among parties "not related parties as per Ind AS 24" [FACT: FIL-C022]. Investors should apply SEBI promoter-group treatment [JUDGMENT].

**Holdco incentive (T5).**
- Bharti Telecom, with BEHPL and BEL, had gross debt of about INR 39,975 crore at 18-Feb-2026. CRISIL expects about INR 46,000 crore after funding the partly paid call. Interest is serviced from Airtel dividends, and the holdco's FY2025 standalone PAT was negative [FACT: FIL-C048].
- This creates a structural preference for steady, rising Airtel dividends and for share-based rather than cash transactions with the promoter group [INFERENCE: FIL-C048].
- The Airtel shares held by the holdco are unencumbered, which removes pledge-driven forced-sale risk [FACT: FIL-C048].

## Consolidation and Subsidiaries

**Conclusion.** Consolidation materially changes the picture. In FY2026:
- About 49% of consolidated profit for the year came from entities other than the standalone parent [FACT: FIL-C056].
- NCI took INR 71,276 mn of profit: Airtel Africa 34,355, Indus 29,514, Hexacom 5,199 [FACT: FIL-C055].
- Group ownership at 31-Mar-2026: Airtel Africa 62.62% (78.93% after the ICIL swap), Indus 51.256%, Hexacom 70%, Nxtra 75.96% [FACT: FIL-C055; FIL-C063].

**Distortions to note:**
1. **FY2025 Indus step-acquisition.** It created a one-time gain and goodwill, and it lifted revenue growth in FY2025-FY2026 [FACT: FIL-C017; CALCULATION: FIL-C069].
