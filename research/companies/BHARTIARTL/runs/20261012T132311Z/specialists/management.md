# Management and Governance — Bharti Airtel Limited (BHARTIARTL)

## Mandate, Scope and Independence

**Decision questions answered:** (1) operational competence of leadership, judged on decisions and outcomes; (2) integrity; (3) governance and protection of minority holders; (4) capital allocation over 10+ years, measured per share; (5) alignment through ownership, pledging, insider transactions and pay; (6) continuity, succession and key-person risk.

**In scope:** the promoter group and holding structure (Bharti Telecom Limited (BTL), Indian Continent Investment Limited (ICIL), Pastel/Viridian (Singtel)); the leadership map and departures; 39 quarterly shareholding patterns; promoter sales and purchases; remuneration for FY2017–FY2026; board composition and committees; auditor history; the capital-allocation record (equity raises, preferential issues, swaps and dividends); regulatory actions; succession.

**Out of scope:** the promise-to-delivery database (concalls), accounting forensics and related-party accounting (filings), market liquidity and TSR (valuation), and segment economics (financials). Where I relied on figures that another domain owns (net income, EPS), I cited the primary disclosure myself and list the dependency for the CIO.

## Key Findings

1. **Promoter control is stable and unencumbered, but the promoters have been net sellers.** The promoter group held 68.29% in Mar-2011 [FACT: MGT-C022] and 67.14% in Mar-2019 [FACT: MGT-C023]. It held 48.87% in Dec-2025 [FACT: MGT-C011] and, after the ICIL swap, 50.07% in Jun-2026 [FACT: MGT-C008]. No pledge, non-disposal undertaking or other encumbrance appeared in any of the 39 patterns retrieved [FACT: MGT-C025; MGT-C009]. Between Dec-2024 and Dec-2025 the promoter group sold a net 255.4 million shares, 7.9% of its stake [CALCULATION: MGT-C046], through block deals at about ₹1,814–2,030 by Pastel (Singtel) [FACT: MGT-C052] and about ₹1,660–1,871 by ICIL (Mittal family) [FACT: MGT-C051]. BTL was a net buyer of 85.1 million shares over FY2025 [CALCULATION: MGT-C027].
2. **The most important governance event in the window is the June-2026 related-party swap.** Airtel issued 146,761,335 new shares (2.35% of post-issue capital) to ICIL, a promoter-group entity, for up to 16.31% of Airtel Africa plc. Airtel's effective stake in Airtel Africa rose to about 79% [FACT: MGT-C006; MGT-C007; CALCULATION: MGT-C046]. The reported terms favor Airtel's minority holders on market prices. Airtel shares were issued at ₹1,923, about 9.5% above market, and Airtel Africa shares were taken at GBP 3.659, about 11.6% below market [FACT: MGT-C049, tier 6, M]. Public institutions voted 99.97% in favor [FACT: MGT-C050, tier 6, M]. The open issue is a corporate-opportunity question. ICIL accumulated part of its Airtel Africa stake in the market in 2024 at about GBP 1.07 [FACT: MGT-C056, tier 6, L] and sold it to the listed parent two years later at a far higher, though below-market, value. Implied consideration was about ₹28,222 crore [CALCULATION: MGT-C048].
3. **Capital allocation from FY2016 to FY2026 created a large aggregate value but diluted per-share holders by 56%.** The share count rose from 3,997.4 million to 6,240.5 million, a 4.3% annual dilution rate [CALCULATION: MGT-C042]. About ₹1.03 lakh crore of equity was issued, roughly ₹72,600 crore of it in cash [CALCULATION: MGT-C044]. The price still compounded at 16.7% a year, unadjusted and excluding dividends [CALCULATION: MGT-C043]. Cumulative consolidated net income over FY2017–FY2026 was only about ₹38,400 crore and was almost fully distributed, so the "value per rupee retained" test cannot be applied [ESTIMATE: MGT-C045]. The equity raised in 2019–2021 was the survival and investment capital through the price war and the AGR liability [INFERENCE: MGT-C033; MGT-C034; MGT-C038].
4. **The CEO transition was planned and executed in an orderly way.** The Board approved a succession plan in Oct-2024. Gopal Vittal (with the company since Apr-2012 [FACT: MGT-C067]) became Executive Vice Chairman and Shashwat Sharma became MD & CEO (Airtel India), both w.e.f. 1-Jan-2026, with shareholder approval on 1-Feb-2026 [FACT: MGT-C003]. The CFO roles were split at the same time: Soumen Ray became Group CFO and Akhil Garg CFO India [FACT: MGT-C004]. The auditor will rotate to S.R. Batliboi in 2027, at the end of Deloitte's maximum tenure [FACT: MGT-C005; MGT-C058].
5. **The founder Chairman is being re-appointed to Sep-2031 at about ₹30 crore from Airtel plus up to GBP 2.2 million from a UK subsidiary.** This is a two-company pay structure that minorities must look through [FACT: MGT-C030; MGT-C029; MGT-C028]. His pay was 382× the median employee's in FY2026 [FACT: MGT-C017]. As a share of profit it is small: the two executive directors' Airtel cash pay was 0.21% of FY2026 consolidated net income [CALCULATION: MGT-C060]. CEO cash pay grew 9.9% a year over FY2017–FY2026, but ESOP exercise gains (₹184.7 crore over the period, ₹80.2 crore in FY2026) dominate the CEO's realized pay [CALCULATION: MGT-C060]. Executive LTIs vest 100% on performance metrics that include relative TSR and operating free cash flow [FACT: MGT-C014].
6. **The board's key committees are fully independent and attendance is high.** The Audit Committee and the HR & Nomination Committee consist only of independent directors, and board attendance was 98% in FY2026 [FACT: MGT-C057]. A Lead Independent Director has been in place since Mar-2024 [FACT: MGT-C031]. The three non-executive non-independent seats are held by Chua Sock Koong and Tao Yih Arthur Lang (Singtel-associated, an inference from the shareholders' arrangement between BTL and Pastel) and Rajan Bharti Mittal, alongside the executive Chairman [FACT: MGT-C019; MGT-C057].
7. **One proven integrity lapse sits in a subsidiary: Airtel Payments Bank, 2017–18.** RBI imposed a ₹5 crore penalty for KYC breaches after complaints that accounts were opened without clear customer consent [FACT: MGT-C054]. UIDAI separately suspended Airtel's Aadhaar e-KYC [FACT: MGT-C055, tier 6]. I found no SEBI order against the company or its leaders in the sources searched. That search was limited (see missing evidence).

## Promoter Group and Holding Structure

**Conclusion:** control sits with Bharti Telecom Limited (BTL), the listed company's promoter. BTL is a joint vehicle whose holding company is Bharti Enterprises (Holding) Private Limited (Mittal family). Singtel holds a separate direct stake through Pastel, and the Mittal family a separate direct stake through ICIL. The structure gives the Mittal family control and Singtel a large economic interest. Neither promoter vehicle has pledged Airtel shares [FACT: MGT-C008; MGT-C010; MGT-C025].

**Group entities that transact with Airtel.** The material related-party counterparties disclosed for FY2026 are Indus Towers (subsidiary; AOC-2 material RPT; a 2026 AGM resolution sought approval of up to ₹26,000 crore a year) and Dixon Electro Appliances (an associate in which Airtel's subsidiaries hold about 47.59%; ₹3,095 crore of FY2026 transactions; up to ₹6,500 crore sought) [FACT: MGT-C032]. Both counterparties are inside the Airtel group perimeter, not promoter-owned entities, so these large RPTs are not a direct leakage channel to the promoters [INFERENCE: MGT-C032]. The Board's report states that all RPTs were at arm's length, that interested directors recuse themselves, and that no financial assistance to promoter-group entities was written off in the last three years [MANAGEMENT STATEMENT: MGT-C062].

**Holdco-level pledges and leverage.** Airtel's shareholding patterns would show any share-backed BTL or ICIL financing as encumbrance, and they show none in any retrieved quarter [FACT: MGT-C025]. BTL has bought Airtel shares in the market (85.1 million in FY2025) [CALCULATION: MGT-C027], and the promoter holdings are funded largely through dividends from Airtel. The Dividend Policy commits Airtel to pass through 100% of the dividends it receives from subsidiaries and associates [FACT: MGT-C059]. BTL's own debt, its rating rationale and its dividend dependence were **not retrieved** (missing evidence). A leveraged BTL would push Airtel toward higher payouts. That pressure is aligned with minority holders on cash returns but could conflict with reinvestment needs in a capex upcycle [INFERENCE].

**Structural changes over time** (from the shareholding series):

- 2019–2021: dilution through the 2019 rights issue (promoters took about 70% of their entitlement [CALCULATION: MGT-C047]), the Jan-2020 QIP and FCCB, a 150 million share BTL sale in May-2020 [FACT: MGT-C013; MGT-C053], and the 2021 rights issue. The promoter stake fell to 55.93% [FACT: MGT-C024].
- 2022–2025: Google's preferential allotment (71.2 million shares) [FACT: MGT-C036], Singtel and ICIL sales, and BTL purchases. The stake reached 52.42% by Mar-2025 and 48.87% by Dec-2025 [FACT: MGT-C024; MGT-C011].
- 2026: the ICIL swap restored the stake to 50.07% [FACT: MGT-C008].

## Incentives and Remuneration

**Conclusion:** pay is high in absolute terms and against the median employee, but small against profit. The design ties the professional CEO's realized pay mostly to share-price performance through ESOPs with 100% performance vesting. The founder's pay is a fixed-plus-bonus package split between Airtel and a UK subsidiary, which reduces transparency without, on current evidence, extracting material value [JUDGMENT: MGT-C014; MGT-C028; MGT-C029; MGT-C060].

- Vittal's cash pay grew at 9.9% a year over FY2017–FY2026 [CALCULATION: MGT-C060]. **Could the incentives reward behavior that harms per-share value?** The LTI scorecard includes revenue market share growth, which can be bought with price cuts or acquisitions. Equity raises that dilute per-share value would not hurt a scorecard measured on aggregate operating free cash flow [INFERENCE: MGT-C014]. The offset is relative TSR, a per-share measure. The weights were not retrieved (missing evidence). The Chairman's criteria (strategy, partnerships, governance) are qualitative and set by an all-independent HR & Nomination Committee [FACT: MGT-C057], but they are not per-share metrics [JUDGMENT].

## Board Quality and Minority Protection

**Conclusion:** formal independence meets the requirement, and the substance looks reasonable: all-independent audit and nomination committees, a Lead Independent Director, regular refresh, and statutory auditor rotation. The principal minority-protection risk is structural. Two promoter blocs (the Mittal family and Singtel) have a shareholders' arrangement that is now embedded in Airtel's Articles, and the family's investment vehicle transacts in the shares of Airtel's listed subsidiary [FACT: MGT-C066; MGT-C056; JUDGMENT].

- **Composition:** 12 directors at 31-Mar-2026: 3 executive (the Chairman, who is the founder and promoter; the Executive Vice Chairman; the MD & CEO), 3 non-executive non-independent, and 6 independent (2 women) [FACT: MGT-C019]. Independents are exactly 50%, the regulatory minimum when the chairman is an executive promoter [CALCULATION: MGT-C065].
- **Committees:** Audit (Mukherjee as chair, Sikri, Narasimhan) and HR & Nomination (Baillie as chair, Khara, Godrej) are fully independent; no executive sits on the Audit Committee [FACT: MGT-C057].
- **Attendance:** 98% in FY2026 [FACT: MGT-C057].
- **Independent-director tenure:** the longest-serving ID (Narasimhan) joined in Mar-2019; the others were appointed in 2021–2025. No ID has a long tenure that would erode independence [FACT: MGT-C067; MGT-C019] (appointment dates in workpaper `leadership_events.md`).
- **Auditor:** rotation happens only at statutory maximums (S.R. Batliboi to 2017, Deloitte to 2027, then S.R. Batliboi again) [FACT: MGT-C058; MGT-C005]. The FY2026 Board's Report responds to auditor (CARO) comments on fixed-asset verification of network equipment, CPE and fibre, and on pending title-deed mutation, all routine for a telecom operator (same Board's Report section as [FACT: MGT-C005]).
- **Related-party transactions:** the material RPTs are with group entities (Indus Towers; the Dixon associate) and are approved by shareholders under Reg. 23 [FACT: MGT-C032]. Management states that all RPTs were at arm's length, with recusal of interested directors [MANAGEMENT STATEMENT: MGT-C062].
- **Voting record:** the ICIL swap EGM passed with 99.97% of public institutional votes in favor [FACT: MGT-C050]. AGM voting results for 2026 and earlier years, including any dissent on the Chairman's pay, could not be downloaded (missing evidence).
- **Minority treatment in capital raises:** two of the three large equity raises were pro-rata rights issues [FACT: MGT-C033; MGT-C041]. The QIP and the Google allotment were priced against market [FACT: MGT-C034; MGT-C036].
- **The ICIL swap as a test case.** In its favor: the issue was priced above market, the target was taken below market, an independent valuer reported, and it was approved by an EGM with institutional support [FACT: MGT-C049; MGT-C050]. Against it: the promoter family's vehicle bought Airtel Africa shares in the market at about GBP 1.07 in 2024 [FACT: MGT-C056]. Airtel, the listed parent, could have bought them itself. Two years later the family sold them to Airtel for new Airtel shares at about GBP 3.66 each, while also monetizing Airtel shares in 2025 [FACT: MGT-C051]. That is a classic corporate-opportunity question. It does not prove a transfer of value from minorities, because the sale was below market, but it calls for an answer to why Airtel did not buy at the 2024 lows. Airtel's own capacity in 2024 (leverage, Africa FX risk) is a plausible alternative explanation [INFERENCE: MGT-C056; MGT-C006].

**Governance classification:** *Clean / Standard* with one *Some concerns* item (the ICIL/Airtel Africa related-party pattern) [JUDGMENT].

## Four-Layer Evidence Crosswalk

### Layer 1 — Bedrock Compounder
- **Relevant evidence:** the 10-year per-share record, retention economics, allocator discipline and succession.
- **Supports:** price CAGR of 16.7% over 10.5 years despite 4.3% annual dilution [CALCULATION: MGT-C043; MGT-C042]; dividends rising with cash generation [FACT: MGT-C040]; orderly professional succession [FACT: MGT-C003]; promoters never pledged [FACT: MGT-C025].
- **Weakens:** book value per share grew only 3.9% a year [CALCULATION: MGT-C065]; near-zero retained earnings over 10 years, so the record is not one of compounding retained capital [ESTIMATE: MGT-C045]; 56% dilution [CALCULATION: MGT-C042].
- **Domain implications:** management can allocate through a cycle and raise capital at the trough, but the past decade was a repair and consolidation phase, not a compounding one. The Layer 1 case depends on the next decade's reinvestment returns.
- **Missing information:** incremental ROIC on the 2019–2021 capital (financials); Africa returns since 2010.
- **Plausibility effect:** No change — governance and stewardship are adequate for Layer 1, but the per-share retention record does not yet show compounding.

### Layer 2 — High-Growth Business
- **Relevant evidence:** the organization's capacity to scale new businesses (home broadband, enterprise, data centers, Africa) and its dilution appetite.
- **Supports:** the leadership bench and execution through 4G/5G rollouts; growth capital raised in pro-rata form [FACT: MGT-C041; MGT-C033].
- **Weakens:** a history of external equity funding (₹1.03 lakh crore issued) [CALCULATION: MGT-C044]; the LTI scorecard includes revenue market share, which can reward growth over per-share value [FACT: MGT-C014].
- **Domain implications:** management will dilute to fund strategic growth when it judges the opportunity large. Investors should expect equity to be one of the funding tools.
- **Missing information:** LTI scorecard weights; plans for further listings or IPOs of subsidiaries.
- **Plausibility effect:** Insufficient evidence — the management domain cannot establish growth economics.

### Layer 3 — Cyclical / Event-Driven Opportunity
- **Relevant evidence:** the willingness to restructure and simplify the listed-subsidiary structure, and the record through cycles.
- **Supports:** the ICIL swap raising the Airtel Africa stake to about 79% at a reported discount to market [FACT: MGT-C006; MGT-C049]; Indus moved from joint venture to subsidiary [FACT: MGT-C064]; the 2026 call money closed the partly-paid overhang [FACT: MGT-C020]; survival capital raised in 2019–2020 [FACT: MGT-C033; MGT-C034].
- **Weakens:** promoter-family transactions in the subsidiaries' shares create conflict-of-interest risk in future simplifications [FACT: MGT-C056]; promoter sell-downs in 2025 added supply overhang [CALCULATION: MGT-C046].
- **Domain implications:** management has an active simplification agenda (Africa consolidation, Indus control). Holdco/SOTP discount-closure mechanisms exist but are promoter-mediated.
- **Missing information:** swap valuation report; further planned group simplifications; BTL leverage.
- **Plausibility effect:** Raises — management has shown willingness and capacity to execute value-realizing restructurings.

### Layer 4 — Venture-Style / Optionality Bet
- **Relevant evidence:** governance safeguards around adjacencies (Payments Bank, Airtel Money, Xtelify, Nxtra data centers).
- **Supports:** willingness to build adjacencies within the group [FACT: MGT-C006].
- **Weakens:** the Payments Bank conduct lapse shows control weakness in a new venture [FACT: MGT-C054].
- **Domain implications:** optionality is held inside a large company with sound governance, so this domain gives no reason to treat the stock as Layer 4.
- **Missing information:** governance of the Airtel Money IPO and Nxtra.
- **Plausibility effect:** No change — management evidence does not alter the Layer 4 case.
