## Executive Investment Thesis

**What the company does.**
- Bharti Airtel is India's second-largest mobile operator by revenue. It took 39.7% of TRAI access-services revenue (AGR) in QE Mar-2026, behind Jio's 44.8% [CALCULATION: CIO-C046].
- It also runs home broadband, enterprise connectivity (Airtel Business), DTH and data centres (Nxtra).
- Through listed subsidiaries it owns towers (Indus Towers, 51.3%) and a 14-country African operator (Airtel Africa, about 79%) [FACT: CIO-C007; CIO-C037; FIL-C055].

**How it makes money.**
- Revenue is subscribers × ARPU on a spectrum-gated, high-fixed-cost network. Incremental ARPU falls through to EBITDA at roughly 90% [ESTIMATE: IND-C067].
- FY2026: consolidated revenue INR 2,10,973 crore [FACT: CIO-C010]; EBITDA margin 56.7% [CALCULATION: FIN-C021], with India Mobile at 60.2% [FACT: IND-C055]; operating cash flow INR 1,22,230 crore [FACT: CIO-C015].

**Why it might create per-share value.**
- India mobile has consolidated to three private operators plus BSNL. CR3 of access AGR rose from 76.5% (FY2013) to 95.8% (FY2026) [CALCULATION: IND-C035].
- The industry has executed three tariff repairs since 2019. Each lifted Airtel's ARPU by about 14–16% [CALCULATION: IND-C066].
- With 5G mobile capex past its peak, FY2026 delivered the first large equity free cash flow, INR 53,054 crore [CALCULATION: FIN-C032].
- Leverage fell to 1.29x net debt (including leases) to EBITDA [FACT: CIO-C039], and the dividend rose 50% to INR 24 [FACT: CON-C162].

**Why the market might misprice it, and in which direction.** We think the market prices in a longer competitive-advantage period and a lower discount rate than the evidence supports.
- The price implies a cost of equity of about 9.9% on base operations [CALCULATION: VAL-C052].
- Our rate is 11.68%, built from a 7.38% G-Sec [FACT: CIO-C047] and Damodaran's 7.08% ERP net of the 1.87% default spread [FACT: CIO-C048].
- The 34-analyst consensus has EPS at INR 82 by FY2028 [ESTIMATE: BEAR-C040]. That needs roughly 16% annual EBITDA growth [CALCULATION: BEAR-C056].

**What could invalidate this view.**
- Two or more tariff rounds within 24 months with no churn, plus falling capex intensity. The two-round DCF point is INR 1,519 [CALCULATION: BEAR-C085], and the bull range is INR 1,690–2,123 [CALCULATION: VAL-C046].
- A structurally lower Indian rate regime that keeps the market multiple near today's 10.9x [INFERENCE: CIO-C059].

**Is today's price attractive on a risk-adjusted basis?** No.
- The margin of safety is −26% [CALCULATION: CIO-C057]. Probability-weighted asymmetry, measured against the hurdle, is 0.06 [CALCULATION: CIO-C057].
- The probability of permanent loss is low but not trivial: 10–20% for a loss of 30% or more, mainly through a competitive reset [ESTIMATE: BEAR-C058; CALCULATION: BEAR-C060].
- The investor is paid less than the risk-free rate for bearing equity risk, unless the market keeps paying today's multiple [JUDGMENT].

## Business and Industry Economics

**Conclusion.** India mobile is a spectrum-gated, high-fixed-cost network business whose returns are set mainly by industry pricing discipline. The structure has improved markedly since 2019, but the record of rational behaviour is about five years long and depends on the conduct of one larger rival [JUDGMENT: IND-C035; IND-C049].

**Engine.**
- Revenue = subscribers × ARPU. About 92% of the base is prepaid [FACT: IND-C054].
- ARPU in Q4FY2026 was INR 257, against INR 214 at Jio and INR 190 at Vi [FACT: CIO-C038; IND-C062; IND-C063]. The premium comes from customer mix, not list price [INFERENCE: IND-C062].

## Final Verdict

**Business quality:** 61/100 (Medium). Good, but with an identifiable structural weakness: through-cycle returns below the cost of capital, and a moat shared with Jio.

**Layer fit:** primary Layer 1 — Bedrock Compounder (52, plausible); secondary Layer 3 — Cyclical / Event-Driven Opportunity (44); Layer 2 — High-Growth Business 30; Layer 4 — Venture-Style / Optionality Bet 12.

**Current valuation:** Expensive. At INR 1,778.70 the price is 26% above the base midpoint (INR 1,306–1,509, one tariff round included) and 17% above a two-round value. EV/EBITDA is 10.9x, the 71st percentile, on record margins.

**Expected return:** about 6.2% a year over 5 years (E[IRR]), against an 11.68% hurdle and a 7.38% G-Sec. If today's multiple holds, it is roughly the hurdle.

**Downside risk:** bear-case loss of about 33% including dividends. The probability of a permanent loss of 30% or more is low (5–20%), mainly through a competitive reset in Indian mobile.

**Evidence confidence:** Medium. 808 claims across 11 ledgers, mostly primary; 19 decision-critical claims re-verified and 5 calculations recomputed without error; an adversarial review incorporated. The main uncertainties are the discount rate, whether the multiple holds, and tariff timing.

**Verdict:** **AVOID** (IA 38; mapped verdict, no override).

The evidence supports this much and no more. Airtel is a better business than at any time in the last decade. At INR 1,778.70, though, fresh capital is expected to earn less than the risk-free rate unless the market keeps paying today's multiple, and to earn more than the hurdle only if tariffs and capex both move in its favour. On current evidence:
- below about INR 1,600 the verdict moves to HOLD / NEUTRAL;
- below about INR 1,175 it moves to BUY.

Two or more tariff rounds together with falling capex intensity would also lift the base case toward the price.
