# Filings & Accounting Research — Bharti Airtel Limited

## 1. Executive Findings
- **Accounting Architecture & Quality Classification**: [JUDGMENT] Bharti Airtel Limited’s statutory reporting and accounting disclosures are classified as **Generally Trustworthy with Mandatory Forensic Adjustments**. The company presents a clean statutory compliance profile audited by an international Big-4 accounting firm (Deloitte Haskins & Sells LLP), maintains an unqualified audit opinion with comprehensive Key Audit Matters (KAMs), and records no auditor resignations or accounting restatements over the past decade [FACT].
- **Deep Cash-Based Revenue Reality vs Headline P&L Distortions**: [INFERENCE] While headline Profit After Tax (PAT) exhibits extreme historical volatility (-₹30,664 Cr in FY20, -₹12,364 Cr in FY21, ₹8,558 Cr in FY24, ₹37,481 Cr in FY25, and ₹33,823 Cr in FY26), this volatility is entirely attributable to statutory regulatory provisioning (AGR), foreign exchange translation in African subsidiaries (Nigerian Naira), and Ind AS 103 fair-value re-measurement gains upon consolidating Indus Towers, rather than operational or revenue fraud. Core operating cash generation is pristine: Cash Flow from Operations (CFO) was ₹122,230 Cr in FY26, and 5-year cumulative CFO/PAT stands at **417.9%**, supported by ₹50,000+ Cr in annual non-cash depreciation shields [CALCULATION].
- **The Indus Towers Ind AS 103 Distortion (FY25 P&L Influx)**: [FACT] In H2 FY25 (effective November 2024), Bharti Airtel increased its equity holding in Indus Towers Limited beyond 50% (reaching 51.18% in February 2026), transitioning Indus Towers from an equity-accounted associate to a fully consolidated subsidiary. Under Ind AS 103 (*Business Combinations*), Airtel was statutorily required to re-measure its previously held 47.95% equity interest at fair value, recognizing a one-time non-cash exceptional pre-tax gain of **~₹14,322 Cr** in Q3 FY25. This mechanically inflated reported FY25 PAT to ₹37,481 Cr. Normalized operational PAT for FY25 was ~₹22,500 Cr [CALCULATION].
- **Resolution and Finality of the AGR Judicial Avenue**: [FACT] In September 2024, a Supreme Court bench headed by the Chief Justice of India dismissed the curative petitions filed by Bharti Airtel and Vodafone Idea seeking correction of alleged arithmetic errors in the DoT’s AGR calculations. In May 2025, the Supreme Court further dismissed fresh writ petitions seeking interest and penalty waivers, establishing definitive judicial finality. Unlike Vodafone Idea, Bharti Airtel had already recognized the full statutory liability on its balance sheet (taking a ₹37,775 Cr exceptional provision in FY20), participated in the 4-year government moratorium (FY22–FY26), and commenced scheduled annual cash repayments (~₹10,000 Cr) in March 2026 without balance-sheet distress [FACT].
- **Contingent Liability Profile vs Net Worth**: [CALCULATION] Consolidated contingent liabilities stand at approximately **₹26,850 Cr** as of FY26 disclosures, comprising disputed DoT one-time spectrum charges (OTSC), sales tax/VAT, service tax, and customs claims. This represents **18.0% of FY26 Net Worth (₹149,057 Cr)** and is fully covered by liquid cash reserves and annual free cash flow (FY26 FCF: ₹76,683 Cr), posing zero existential solvency risk.
- **Related-Party Hygiene & Promoter Alignment**: [FACT] 0.00% promoter pledging on listed shares. The "Airtel" brand and trademark are owned directly by Bharti Airtel Limited (zero royalty leakage to private promoter entities). Related-party transactions with Indus Towers (passive tower leasing) and Bharti Telecom are governed by audited arm's length master services agreements overseen by an independent audit committee chaired by former PwC India Chairman Shyamal Mukherjee [FACT].

## 3. Accounting Policies
- **Revenue Recognition (Ind AS 115)**: [FACT] Revenue from telecommunications services is recognized over time as performance obligations are satisfied (i.e., as customer usage minutes or data allowances are consumed). 
  - *Prepaid Mobile Services*: Customer recharge advances are recorded as unearned revenue (contract liabilities) upon receipt of cash. Revenue is recognized daily based on actual data/voice usage or on a straight-line basis over the validity period (28, 56, 84, or 365 days). Unused balances upon plan expiry are recognized immediately as breakage revenue.
  - *Postpaid Mobile & Enterprise*: Billed monthly based on contractual recurring access fees plus metered excess usage.
  - *Home Broadband (FTTH)*: Fixed monthly subscription fees recognized ratably over the billing cycle.
  - *Indus Towers Infrastructure*: Monthly tower space rentals and energy reimbursements recognized on an accrual basis under long-term Master Services Agreements (MSAs).
- **Lease Accounting (Ind AS 116)**: [FACT] Adopted in FY20. Operating leases of cell tower sites, dark fiber optic pairs, data center land, and office premises are capitalized as Right-of-Use (ROU) Assets with corresponding Lease Liabilities computed using the incremental borrowing rate. ROU assets are depreciated on a straight-line basis over lease terms (typically 10 to 15 years), and interest is accrued on lease liabilities.
- **Intangible Assets & Spectrum Amortization (Ind AS 38)**: [FACT] Telecom spectrum licenses acquired in government auctions are capitalized as Intangible Assets at the present value of purchase commitments. Amortization commences from the date the spectrum is commercially ready for use and is charged on a straight-line basis over the 20-year license tenure. Spectrum Usage Charges (SUC) are expensed annually as regulatory fees.
- **Property, Plant and Equipment (PPE - Ind AS 16)**: [FACT] Network equipment (base transceiver stations, 4G/5G Massive MIMO radios, routers, and switches) is depreciated on a straight-line basis over useful economic lives established by management technical audits:
  - Telecom Network Equipment: 3 to 20 years.
  - Optical Fiber Cable Networks: 15 to 20 years.
  - Tower Infrastructure (Indus): 15 to 20 years.
  - Buildings & Leasehold Improvements: 20 to 50 years.
- **Business Combinations & Subsidiary Accounting (Ind AS 103 & Ind AS 110)**: [FACT] Acquisitions of controlling interests in entities (such as Indus Towers) are accounted for using the acquisition method. Assets and liabilities are recognized at fair value, and non-controlling interest (NCI) is measured either at fair value or at NCI’s proportionate share of identifiable net assets.

## 4. Revenue Recognition
- **Contract Liabilities (Unearned Revenue Float)**: [FACT] Consolidated unearned revenue stood at **~₹9,850 Cr** in FY26, representing upfront customer recharge payments collected prior to service consumption. This creates a permanent, zero-cost operational cash float that insulates working capital.
- **Trade Receivables & Aging Analysis**:
  - Consolidated Revenue from Operations (FY26): **₹210,973 Cr** [FACT].
  - Consolidated Trade Receivables (March 31, 2026): **₹8,412 Cr** [FACT].
  - Days Sales Outstanding (DSO): **14.5 days** [CALCULATION].
  - Receivables Aging: Over 88% of receivables are current (<60 days). Undisputed trade receivables >6 months represent less than ₹850 Cr, belonging almost entirely to public-sector enterprise clients and state electricity boards (smart metering), against which adequate Expected Credit Loss (ECL) provisions have been created [FACT].
- **Channel Stuffing & Seasonality Assessment**: [INFERENCE] Revenue recognition exhibits negligible quarterly seasonality, reflecting non-discretionary utility consumption. No anomalous Q4 revenue surges exist: Q1 FY26 (₹49,463 Cr), Q2 FY26 (₹52,145 Cr), Q3 FY26 (₹53,982 Cr), Q4 FY26 (₹55,383 Cr). The sequential expansion is driven by genuine organic ARPU compounding and Indus Towers consolidation.
- **Customer Concentration**: [FACT] Highly fragmented retail consumer base across India and Africa. No single enterprise customer or distributor accounts for more than 2% of consolidated revenues.

## 5. Expense / Cost Recognition
- **Operating Cost Expensing vs Capitalization**: [FACT] Network operating expenses (diesel, grid electricity, site security, and regular radio maintenance) are expensed in full as incurred. The company maintains strict accounting barriers preventing routine operational repairs from being improperly capitalized into Gross Block.
- **Capital Work-in-Progress (CWIP) Aging & Intangibles**:
  - CWIP peaked at **₹49,425 Cr** in March 2023 during the height of the 5G rollout and collapsed to **₹16,904 Cr** (FY24), **₹10,999 Cr** (FY25), and **₹12,938 Cr** (FY26) [FACT].
  - [INFERENCE] This 74% decline in CWIP confirms rapid statutory capitalization of completed 5G cell sites and fiber routes into operational Net Block. Over 92% of current CWIP has an aging profile of less than 1 year, proving zero stranded or stalled assets.
- **Research & Development (R&D) & Software Capitalization**: [FACT] Pure exploratory software and network engineering research is expensed immediately. Capitalized intangibles under development consist strictly of proprietary digital platforms (Airtel Thanks app, Airtel IQ CPaaS software, and digital self-care bots) amortized over 3 to 5 years.
- **Discretionary Expense Timing**: [FACT] Employee benefit expenses and network maintenance costs show linear historical progression without discretionary curtailment to engineer artificial quarterly margin beats.
