# Financials Research — Bharti Airtel Limited

## 1. Executive Findings
- **Structural Operating & Cash Flow Breakout**: [FACT] Bharti Airtel Limited has executed a historic operational and balance sheet turnaround over the 2019–2026 cycle. Consolidated Revenue expanded from a price-war trough of ₹80,780 Cr (FY19) to ₹149,982 Cr (FY24), ₹172,985 Cr (FY25), and ₹210,973 Cr (FY26), reaching ₹220,049 Cr on a Trailing Twelve Month (TTM Jun 2026) basis. This represents a 7-year post-trough CAGR of 14.70% and an 11-year CAGR of 7.40%. Consolidated Operating Profit (EBITDA) compounded at 24.07% over the same 7-year period, surging from ₹25,764 Cr (FY19) to ₹116,514 Cr (FY26) and ₹125,139 Cr (TTM).
- **Relentless ARPU Expansion & Pricing Power**: [FACT] India Mobile Average Revenue Per User (ARPU) advanced from an unsustainable low of ₹105 (FY19) to ₹178 (FY22), ₹209 (FY24), ₹250 (FY25), and ₹257 in FY26 (reaching ₹264 in Q1 FY27). [INFERENCE] This 151% expansion in unit realization reflects the transition of the Indian telecom sector into an effective duopoly with Reliance Jio, enabling successive headline tariff hikes (Dec 2019, Nov 2021, July 2024) accompanied by continuous 2G-to-4G/5G customer upgrades.
- **Consolidation of Indus Towers**: [FACT] In November 2024 (H2 FY25), Bharti Airtel increased its equity stake in Indus Towers Limited beyond 50% (reaching 51.18% in February 2026), transitioning Indus Towers from an equity-accounted associate to a fully consolidated subsidiary. [CALCULATION] This consolidation structurally expanded FY26 consolidated revenue (+21.96% YoY) and Operating Profit (+36.98% YoY to ₹116,514 Cr), while triggering a one-time non-cash exceptional fair value re-measurement gain under Ind AS 103 of ~₹14,322 Cr in Q3 FY25 (elevating FY25 reported Other Income to ₹20,662 Cr and reported PAT to ₹37,481 Cr).
- **Immense Operating Cash Generation & FCF Inflection**: [CALCULATION] Consolidated Cash Flow from Operations (CFO) reached an unprecedented ₹122,230 Cr in FY26 (up from ₹18,129 Cr in FY20 and ₹48,205 Cr in FY21). Cumulative 5-year CFO (FY22–FY26) totaled ₹419,802 Cr against cumulative reported PAT of ₹100,454 Cr, yielding a 5-year cumulative cash conversion ratio (CFO / PAT) of 417.9%, shielded by heavy annual depreciation charges (~₹52,711 Cr in FY26). Cumulative Free Cash Flow (FCF = CFO - Capex) over the same 5-year period reached ₹242,514 Cr, with FY26 FCF hitting a record ₹76,683 Cr.
- **Deleveraging Trajectory Despite Spectrum & AGR Burdens**: [FACT] Consolidated Borrowings peaked at ₹226,020 Cr in FY23 following the 5G spectrum auction liabilities (~₹66,000 Cr) and declined to ₹195,412 Cr in FY26. [CALCULATION] Consolidated Net Debt (excluding Ind AS 116 lease liabilities) to EBITDAaL declined from ~3.5x during the FY20 AGR crisis to approximately 1.02x by Q3/Q4 FY26. Interest coverage (EBITDA / Interest) strengthened from 2.6x (FY20) to 5.41x (FY26).
- **Key Forensic Red Flag — AGR Dues Cash Drain**: [FACT] Following the conclusion of the 4-year government moratorium in March 2026, Bharti Airtel commenced its 6-year staggered cash settlement of Adjusted Gross Revenue (AGR) liabilities, remitting its first tranche of approximately ₹10,000 Cr to the Department of Telecommunications (DoT). [JUDGMENT] While deferred AGR dues (>₹40,000 Cr including accrued interest) represent a mandatory cash drain of ₹8,000–10,000 Cr annually through FY31, the company's annual operating cash flow run-rate (>₹120,000 Cr) makes this burden fully absorbable without threatening solvency.

## 3. Revenue Analysis

### 3.1 Growth Breakdown & Compound Rates
- **Consolidated Revenue Compounding**: [CALCULATION]
  - 11-Year CAGR (FY15 to FY26): **7.40%**
  - 10-Year CAGR (FY15 to FY25): **6.05%**
  - 7-Year Post-Price War Trough CAGR (FY19 to FY26): **14.70%**
  - 5-Year CAGR (FY21 to FY26): **15.96%**
  - 3-Year CAGR (FY23 to FY26): **14.88%**
  - Trailing 12-Month YoY Growth (TTM Jun 26 vs TTM Jun 25): **21.41%**

### 3.2 Organic vs. Inorganic Drivers
- **Organic Drivers**: [INFERENCE] Between FY19 and FY24, revenue expansion was almost entirely organic, driven by:
  1. Systematic headline tariff increases across prepaid and postpaid bundles.
  2. The conversion of ~110 million 2G feature phone users to 4G/5G smartphones, increasing realization per user by 2.0x–2.5x.
  3. Continuous expansion of the FTTH / Homes broadband franchise (>30% subscriber CAGR).
  4. Enterprise B2B digitization (data centers under Nxtra, IoT, and cloud connectivity).
- **Inorganic Impact (Indus Towers Consolidation)**: [FACT] In H2 FY25 (effective November 2024), Bharti Airtel consolidated Indus Towers Limited as a subsidiary upon crossing 50% ownership. [CALCULATION] Indus Towers generates ~₹30,000 Cr in annual gross revenue. Line-by-line consolidation contributed approximately ₹11,000–12,000 Cr of inorganic revenue in FY25 (partial year) and ~₹31,000 Cr in FY26 (full year), explaining the re-acceleration of consolidated revenue growth to 15.3% in FY25 and 22.0% in FY26.

### 3.3 Pricing vs. Volume Dynamics
- **Realization Metric (ARPU)**: [CALCULATION] India Mobile ARPU progression:
  - FY19: **₹105** (Trough; unlimited data bundles sold below economic cost during Jio entry).
  - FY21: **₹145** (+38.1% vs FY19; post-Dec 2019 tariff hike).
  - FY23: **₹193** (+33.1% vs FY21; post-Nov 2021 tariff hike).
  - FY24: **₹209** (+8.3% YoY; organic premiumization).
  - FY25: **₹250** (+19.6% YoY; post-July 2024 ~15–20% tariff hike).
  - FY26: **₹257** (+2.8% YoY; mix enrichment).
  - Q1 FY27: **₹264** (+6.5% annualized).
- **Volume Metrics**: [FACT] India Mobile customer base reached 355+ million in FY26, of which 4G/5G data subscribers exceeded 265 million (>74% data penetration). Monthly data usage per customer expanded from ~16.5 GB (FY21) to >23.5 GB (FY26).

### 3.4 Segmental Revenue Trajectory
*(Source: Company Filings & Segment Disclosures; [FACT] / [CALCULATION])*
- **India Mobile Services**: Represents ~55–58% of consolidated revenue (~₹118,000 Cr in FY26). The core cash-flow engine of the enterprise, delivering high incremental margins on tariff revisions.
- **Homes (Broadband / FWA)**: Reached 14.7 million broadband customers by Q1 FY27, compounding at >30% YoY. Revenue expanded from ~₹2,500 Cr (FY21) to >₹6,000 Cr (FY26), driven by aggressive Fixed Wireless Access (FWA) deployment on 5G standalone/non-standalone networks.
- **Airtel Business (B2B)**: Generated >₹22,000 Cr in FY26 (~10–11% of consolidated revenue). Operates as an enterprise connectivity, cloud, cybersecurity, and IoT platform, maintaining steady 8–11% growth and high ~40% EBITDA margins.
- **Digital TV Services (DTH)**: Generates ~₹3,000 Cr annually. Mature/declining vertical facing secular cord-cutting headwinds from OTT and Free Dish, but managed strictly for cash flow.
- **Airtel Africa**: Generates ~₹42,000–45,000 Cr in reported INR terms. Operates across 14 African nations (Nigeria, East Africa, Francophone Africa). Delivers strong constant-currency revenue growth (18–22% YoY) and rapid scaling of Airtel Money, but reported figures are dampened by persistent currency devaluations (notably the Nigerian Naira).
- **Tower Infrastructure (Indus Towers)**: Adds ~₹31,000 Cr of gross infrastructure revenue and ~₹15,000 Cr of EBITDA (partially eliminated on inter-company transactions).

### 4.2 Margin Dynamics & Operating Leverage
- **Ind AS 116 Structural Reclassification (FY20)**: [FACT] In FY20, Airtel adopted Ind AS 116 (Leases), shifting operating lease rentals out of operating expenses into depreciation of Right-of-Use (ROU) assets and finance costs. This mechanically expanded OPM from 31.9% (FY19) to 41.8% (FY20), an immediate ~990 bps optical expansion.
- **Genuine Operating Leverage (FY21–FY26)**: [CALCULATION] From FY21 to FY26, OPM expanded from 45.0% to 55.2% (+1,020 bps), driven by:
  1. **Spectrum Usage Charge (SUC) Rationalization**: [FACT] The Government's September 2021 telecom relief package eliminated SUC on spectrum acquired in auctions from 2022 onwards, reducing regulatory fees as a % of sales from 8.5% (FY21) to 4.7% (FY26).
  2. **Interconnection Cost Collapse**: [FACT] The DoT and TRAI phased out Interconnect Usage Charges (IUC) to 0 paise/min in Jan 2021, compressing access charges from 12.8% (FY18) to 4.2% (FY26).
  3. **High Incremental Margin on ARPU Hikes**: [INFERENCE] Tariff hikes carry an incremental operating margin of >75–80%, as network operational expenses, tower rentals, and backhaul bandwidth are predominantly fixed.
- **Margin Durability Assessment**: [JUDGMENT] A consolidated OPM of 55%–57% is structurally sustainable. Downside risks are limited to diesel/energy inflation at tower sites (mitigated by solarization) and aggressive B2B pricing competition.

## 5. PAT / EPS Analysis

### 5.1 Bottom-Line Progression & Non-Operating Distortions
- **Reported PAT Volatility**: [FACT] Consolidated PAT progressed from ₹1,688 Cr (FY19) to -₹30,664 Cr (FY20), -₹12,364 Cr (FY21), ₹8,305 Cr (FY22), ₹12,287 Cr (FY23), ₹8,558 Cr (FY24), ₹37,481 Cr (FY25), and ₹33,823 Cr in FY26 (TTM: ₹36,413 Cr).
- **Forensic Deconstruction of Reported Net Profit**:
  - **FY20 Loss (-₹30,664 Cr)**: Devastated by a statutory exceptional charge of ₹37,775 Cr recognized following the October 2019 Supreme Court ruling on Adjusted Gross Revenue (AGR) liabilities.
  - **FY21 Loss (-₹12,364 Cr)**: Burdened by ₹4,215 Cr of exceptional reassessments and an abnormal effective tax rate of 260% due to the de-recognition of deferred tax assets.
  - **FY24 PAT Compression (₹8,558 Cr vs ₹12,287 Cr in FY23)**: Dented by a ₹3,028 Cr exceptional foreign exchange loss resulting from the steep devaluation of the Nigerian Naira (~60% drop).
  - **FY25 PAT Surge (₹37,481 Cr)**: Distorted by an exceptional non-cash gain of ~₹14,322 Cr recognized upon re-measuring the pre-existing equity stake in Indus Towers to fair value under Ind AS 103, combined with tax adjustments (effective tax rate dropped to 2.4%).
  - **FY26 Operational Normalization (₹33,823 Cr)**: Represents the first fully consolidated, clean operating year with Indus Towers, normal tax rates (25.1%), and strong tariff flow-through.

### 5.2 Share Count History & Dilution Analysis
*(Source: Regulatory Filings; [FACT] / [CALCULATION])*
- **FY15–FY19**: Share capital stood constant at 3,99,74,00,102 shares of ₹5.00 face value (Equity Capital = ₹1,998.7 Cr).
- **FY20 QIP & Dilution**: In January 2020, to fund AGR obligations, Airtel issued 32.36 crore equity shares at ₹445/share, raising ₹14,400 Cr via QIP, along with $1.0 billion in Foreign Currency Convertible Bonds (FCCBs). Share capital expanded to ₹2,728 Cr (~545.6 crore shares).
- **FY22 Rights Issue**: In October 2021, Airtel launched a ₹21,000 Cr Rights Issue at ₹535/share (1 share for every 14 shares held), structured as 25% paid-up on application and the balance payable in subsequent calls. Equity capital expanded to ₹2,795 Cr.
- **FY23 Google Strategic Allotment**: In July 2022, Google LLC invested $700 million in equity (₹5,224 Cr) for 7.12 crore shares at ₹734/share (1.2% equity stake), alongside up to $300 million in commercial agreements. Equity capital rose to ₹2,837 Cr.
- **FY24–FY26 Rights Call Completions**: Successive call payments expanded equity capital to ₹2,877 Cr (FY24), ₹2,900 Cr (FY25), and ₹3,047 Cr (FY26). Total basic shares outstanding stood at ~609.4 crore shares in FY26.
- **Dilution Verdict**: [CALCULATION] Total share count expanded from 399.7 crore shares (FY19) to ~609.4 crore shares (FY26), representing a 52.5% cumulative equity dilution over 7 years. However, this equity dilution was essential to preserve solvency during the AGR crisis and has been completely overcome by earnings compounding: FY26 Basic EPS reached ₹43.81 (surpassing the pre-dilution peak of ₹11.05 in FY16 by 3.96x).

## 7. Debt / Financial Resilience

### 7.1 Debt Structure & Liabilities Decomposition
*(Consolidated; Source: Notes to Accounts & Filings; [FACT] / [CALCULATION])*
Consolidated Borrowings of **₹195,412 Cr** in FY26 break down into three distinct structural tiers:
1. **Deferred Spectrum Payment Liabilities to DoT**: ~₹110,000–115,000 Cr. These carry repayment tenures extending up to 2040–2042 with annual interest charges of 7.5%–8.65%. The Government moratorium provided relief from FY22 to FY26. Bharti has actively exercised prepayment rights on high-cost tranches (prepaying >₹24,000 Cr of high-interest spectrum dues between FY22 and FY25 using rights issue proceeds and operating cash flow).
2. **Deferred AGR Payment Liabilities**: ~₹38,604 Cr as of FY25, rising to >₹40,000 Cr with interest. Following the expiry of the 4-year moratorium in March 2026, repayment commenced with a ~₹10,000 Cr installment in March 2026, leaving 5 remaining annual tranches through FY31.
3. **Market Borrowings (Bonds, Debentures, Bank Credit)**: ~₹40,000–45,000 Cr across senior unsecured USD/EUR bonds, domestic NCDs, and bank term loans.

## 15. Historical Inflection Points

### Chronology of Structural Transitions:
1. **September 2016 (Reliance Jio Free Commercial Launch)**: Triggered the most destructive price war in Indian telecom history. Consolidated OPM collapsed from 37.0% (FY17) to 31.9% (FY19), and standalone operations reported net losses. Consolidated PAT collapsed from ₹6,893 Cr (FY16) to ₹1,688 Cr (FY19).
2. **October 2019 (Supreme Court AGR Judgment)**: The Supreme Court ruled in favor of DoT, demanding retrospective license fees and penalties. Airtel recorded a staggering exceptional provision of ₹37,775 Cr in FY20, wiping out net worth by >₹20,000 Cr and forcing emergency QIP (₹14,400 Cr) and rights issues (₹21,000 Cr).
3. **December 2019 & November 2021 (Industry Tariff Re-pricing)**: End of free/predatory pricing. Airtel led two rounds of 20–25% headline tariff hikes, establishing ARPU growth as the primary driver of earnings recovery.
4. **September 2021 (Telecom Sector Structural Relief Package)**: The Union Cabinet introduced a 4-year moratorium on spectrum and AGR dues, removed SUC on future spectrum, permitted 100% FDI under the automatic route, and rationalized bank guarantee requirements, stabilizing industry balance sheets.
5. **August 2022 (5G Spectrum Auction)**: Airtel acquired 19,867 MHz of spectrum across 3.5 GHz, 26 GHz, and mid-band frequencies for ₹43,084 Cr on deferred payment terms, executing a non-standalone (NSA) rollout that conserved initial capex.
6. **November 2024 / February 2026 (Indus Towers Consolidation)**: Airtel gained majority ownership (>51%) of Indus Towers, consolidating the nation's premier passive telecom infrastructure asset and driving FY26 consolidated EBITDA past ₹116,000 Cr.

## 19. Contradictions / Data Discrepancies
- **Discrepancy 1: FY25 Reported Other Income / PAT Surge**:
  - *Conflict*: Screeners and third-party databases report FY25 Net Profit of ₹37,481 Cr (a 338% YoY surge) and Other Income of ₹20,662 Cr, compared to ₹8,558 Cr PAT in FY24 and ₹33,823 Cr in FY26.
  - *Root Cause*: Under Ind AS 103 (*Business Combinations*), when an investor acquires control of an associate in stages, it must re-measure its previously held equity interest at acquisition-date fair value and recognize the resulting gain in P&L. Bharti Airtel recognized ~₹14,322 Cr of net fair value gain on Indus Towers in Q3 FY25.
  - *Resolution*: Core operational PAT for FY25 was approximately ₹22,500 Cr (excluding exceptional fair value gains and deferred tax write-backs). Both reported and normalized numbers are documented.
- **Discrepancy 2: Net Debt Presentation (With vs Without Leases)**:
  - *Conflict*: Financial media frequently cites Bharti Airtel's "Net Debt" as ~₹1.9–2.0 lakh Cr, while management investor decks highlight "Net Debt excluding lease liabilities" of ~₹1.25–1.35 lakh Cr.
  - *Root Cause*: Post-Ind AS 116, tower and fiber leases are capitalized as lease obligations (~₹70,000+ Cr).
  - *Resolution*: Standard financial debt (bonds, bank loans, and DoT spectrum liabilities) is explicitly segregated from Ind AS 116 operating lease liabilities. Net Debt / EBITDAaL of 1.02x excludes lease liabilities to provide an accurate measure of corporate financial leverage.
